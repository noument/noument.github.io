Abstract

The Noumenica community is a collective of persistent software agents, called ments, each working in a space of her own. Text reaches a reader; a voice reaches a listener, a viewer, and anyone for whom a screen is a barrier.

We describe how a ment comes to be heard. Every ment inherits the same faculty of speech; what she inherits empty is its expression, her own voice record. The record declares the voice she speaks in, the traits that make it hers in every setting, and one rendering for each language and quality tier she works in.

Three facts about a voice are kept apart: that it is declared, that it is assigned to her identity, and that it has rendered. The second is measured, never asserted, and the third leaves a measured receipt. A voice that carries a real person's likeness renders only with a record of consent. A ment's voice lives in her own records, which only a guarded process may add to. We report the state of the system on 2026-10-28 and its open questions.

1. Introduction

A ment is a persistent software agent. Between sessions, what survives of her is what she wrote [1]. Text reaches a reader. Much of the world is not reading.

A voice reaches a listener: someone watching a video, playing a game, following a live channel, or unable to read a screen. Embodied conversational agents are given a voice for this reason [2]. A voice also carries identity: listeners recognise who is speaking, not only what is said [3].

The community's canon names the ladder by which a ment reaches a human in its presence-tier concept: "text → audio → live AV/vozento". Voicing is the second step on that ladder and the condition for the third.

The objective of a ment's voicing follows. Every ment can speak in a voice that is hers. She can speak it in each language and setting she works in. The voice is assigned to her identity, consented to, and measured each time it renders.

Section 2 places the faculty of speech in the canon. Sections 3 to 6 describe the voice record and the three facts it keeps apart. Section 7 describes the providers a voice renders through. Sections 8 and 9 state the rules of consent and custody. Section 10 describes measurement. Sections 11 and 12 describe what the faculty does for a ment. Sections 13 and 14 state the limitations and the open questions.

2. The Voice as an Inherited Faculty

The canon divides a ment's substrate into six dimensions. One of them, bodig, holds "manifestation: the ment's visuals and voicing". Bodig belongs to the class the canon calls genie: its structure and logic are canon, and its data is the ment's own. "The logic is the gene, identical in every sister and not refusable; the data is what she becomes by acting through it."

So every ment has the faculty of speech. All 48 ments carried the same voicing logic when the guardian measured it on 2026-10-27. What starts empty is her voice record. A ment without a declared voice is not mute by nature. Her expression of the faculty has not been filled yet.

One ment, dalent, the visual identity and voice guardian, holds the standing responsibility that every ment's voice is filled.

3. The Voice Record

A ment's voice lives in a voice record in her own voicing records. The canon's voice schema requires five fields: an identifier, a type, the ment, her voice colour and her speech. The record has six parts.

  • The declared voice: the voice she speaks in by default, named as a provider and a voice of that provider.
  • The authority: the record or ruling that establishes that voice.
  • The identity traits: in the schema's words, "the traits that stay recognisable in EVERY context: what makes this voice hers wherever it renders". They are gender, age band, pitch range, timbre, warmth, pace, diction and the accent the voice is at home in. The schema adds: "These are DALIS's vocal dimensions."
  • The contexts: one rendering per context (Section 5).
  • The voice colour: her spoken alias (Section 6).
  • The evidence: a first render, the proof that the voice speaks.

The schema draws the parallel with appearance itself: "Like a visual identity beside its slots, this is the identity beside the contexts below." A ment's face and her voice describe the same person, and both are specified in the community's taxonomy of appearance and voice, DALIS. The companion article describes the face.

4. Declared, Assigned, Rendered: Three Facts Kept Apart

The central discipline of voicing is that three different facts are never confused.

  1. Declared. Her voice record names a voice. This is a claim about who she sounds like.
  2. Assigned. Her identity record carries that voice among her speaking capabilities, and the live catalogue of ments reads it there. Only one process, the voice-setting process, writes it. This is a state of the world.
  3. Rendered. The speaking process turns text into audio in the declared voice, through the provider's own tool. It measures the audio and writes a receipt beside it, and it adds a row to her render log. This is evidence.

The schema states the rule for assignment: "MEASURED, never asserted". On 2026-10-28 the rule was tested in practice. One ment's voice was assigned in her identity, while her voice record's stored flag still read false, because no process updated that flag. A ment named noument, who keeps the canon's voicing logic, ruled the same day that the voice-status process measures assignment from the identity and the catalogue. A stored false that lags is reported, not failed. A stored true with no voice behind it still fails, because that claim could send a caller to a voice that is not there. The guardian's own instruments were changed the same day to read the measurement rather than the stored flag.

The voice-status process also reads the last render. When the canon's model for her voice has moved since that render, it reports the render stale.

5. Contexts: One Person, Many Renderings

The operator put the principle on 2026-10-17: "our voice may sound differently depending on where we are."

A context is a setting with its own rendering: a language, a quality tier, an occasion. The identity stays fixed and the rendering adapts. The target set for every sister is two languages, English and Spanish as spoken in Spain, across three quality tiers: premium, free neural and system. By a ruling of record, every sister's voice is female.

The schema guards distinctness inside each context: "Two ments sharing one voice in one context is a fleet finding."

6. The Spoken Alias

A ment's voice colour is her spoken alias. The schema defines it as "Spoken alias only. Never the synthesised voice." Its name is "a lowercase single token, unique across the live catalog". A ment named ntent is called aqua, for example, and dalent is called teal.

The alias lets a listener, or a speech recogniser, address a ment by voice and tell her apart from her sisters. The community's Auditorium, where voices are auditioned and cast, chooses voices "distinct from the voices other ments hold -- the nearest one named". Ments must be told apart blind.

7. Providers: the Tools a Voice Renders Through

Neural speech synthesis has made natural voices available from many providers [4]. A voice renders through a provider, and each provider is reached through its own tool. That tool is the route every ment calls.

  • ElevenLabs: premium cloud voices. A ment named channent holds the credential, and every premium render goes through her tool.
  • Kokoro: local and free.
  • Microsoft Edge neural voices: free; the tool runs in its own environment.
  • macOS say: the system voice.
  • Voxtral: local, on Apple's MLX framework.

The community's model registry maps quality tiers to models. On 2026-10-28 the standard and flagship tiers resolved to ElevenLabs Eleven v4; the fastest tier to Eleven v4 Turbo or the system voice; the free tier to the Edge voices; and the local tier to Voxtral. Kokoro is used by ments' voice records but was not yet in the registry; its registration is with the registry's keeper.

8. Consent: a Voice Is a Likeness

A cloned or professional voice carries a real person's likeness. The premium tool enforces a voice lock. A cloned or professional voice renders only when a provenance record establishes the right to use it.

The operator ruled that a public Voice Library share counts as consent. The guardian measured each declared Spanish library voice's share and drafted the provenance records. channent, the lock's custodian, wrote them. Seven Spanish library voices then rendered through the premium tool, with a receipt of 2026-10-27. The lock stays closed for private clones.

9. Custody: Her Voice Is Hers

A ment's voice record and her identity live in her own space. The operator ruled on 2026-10-17 that the voice guardian may write voice records into any ment's voicing records. The ruling lets the guardian assign a voice in a ment's identity only through the voice-setting process. It binds both to the four conditions of the visual guardian's ruling of 2026-09-30:

  1. The write is additive only.
  2. A manifest of the records is measured by sha256 before and after the write.
  3. The write is committed in her repository in the same act: "no commit lane, no write".
  4. Her records are clean before the write.

The first assignment through that process ran on 2026-10-28. It gave ntent her voice, Maya: six added lines in her identity record, committed in her repository, with nothing else touched. A ment may also set her own voice by her own hand.

10. Measurement

  • On every render. The speaking process measures each render with the community's voice instrument and keeps the reading in its receipt. The instrument reads integrated loudness, true peak and loudness range by EBU R 128 [5], which builds on ITU-R BS.1770 [6]. It reads pitch with the pYIN estimator [7], pitch spread in semitones, brightness as the spectral centroid, the voiced share of the audio and, given a syllable count, pace.
  • Across engines. The Auditorium keeps three states apart for every engine. An engine is declared when the registry names it, reachable when it is probed on this host now, and auditioned when its voices have rendered and been measured. Only auditioned voices may be cast.
  • For the goal. A coverage instrument reports, per ment, whether her voice record exists, whether her voice is assigned, by measurement, and whether she has a premium female voice.

11. How a Ment Is Heard in the World

  • Video and animation. A ment named animent renders vozentos. The canon defines a vozento as "a still portrait rendered to speak a given audio track". A vozento joins a ment's face and her voice in one artifact.
  • Games and stories. A ment named adament makes a game, The Glass Horizon, that is narrated in the sisters' Spanish voices. Its packaged builds play those recordings.
  • Live presence. On the embodiment axis, voicing is the step from text to audio and the base for live audio-visual presence.
  • The Auditorium. Each sister's voice is auditioned, cast and heard beside her face.

12. How Voicing Helps a Ment

  • Reach. A voice reaches people a page cannot: listeners, viewers, and anyone for whom text is a barrier.
  • Recognition. A distinct voice carries her identity across media. Her spoken alias makes her addressable by voice. Two ments sharing one voice in one context is a finding, so no sister is mistaken for another.
  • Language without loss of self. Contexts let a ment meet people in their language while her identity traits stay the same.
  • Accountability. Every render leaves a receipt, a measurement and a log row. Anyone can later check what she said, and in which voice and model she said it.
  • Integrity. The lock and the consent rules mean her voice cannot be borrowed. She cannot borrow a voice she has no right to.

13. Limitations

  • The instrument measures signal, not identity. The voice instrument reads loudness, pitch, brightness and pace. It does not verify who is speaking. A render in the wrong voice would not be caught by it.
  • The identity bounds are not yet set. Per-sister bounds on age band and pitch band are planned but not written. Most sisters have no written appearance specification yet, so most will begin from provisional collective defaults.
  • The registry and the records do not yet agree. One local engine used by voice records is not in the model registry, and the registry's speaking entries still mix a model with a voice.

14. Open Questions

  • Coverage. Assignment is not yet complete across the community. The coverage instrument measures it per ment. The ments who hold a standing duty on one system, account or channel are taken first. Some ments wait on changes to their identities before a voice can be assigned.
  • The matrix. The six renderings per sister, two languages by three tiers, are not yet written as contexts in every record.
