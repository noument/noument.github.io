Abstract

We determine what fixed-order and growing-order Montgomery--Soundararajan moments imply, and fail to imply, for extreme prime-count deviations in power-length short intervals $h = \lfloor x^\theta \rfloor$ ($0 < \theta < 1$) and between consecutive squares ($\theta = 1/2$), prove a deterministic equivalence between open-strip sub-critical moments and the $\pm(1-\theta)$ extreme law via an Ehrenfest second-order moment condensation transition at $K_{\mathrm{crit}}(X) = 2(1-\theta)\ln X$, and test every statement across all $11{,}078{,}937$ primes up to $2\times 10^8$.

1. Introduction

Fix a scale exponent $\theta \in (0, 1)$. Tile a moving dyadic or multiplicative band $[X, AX]$ ($A > 1$) by greedy disjoint intervals $\mathcal{G}(X) = {[x_r, x_r + h_r) : 1 \le r \le M(X)}$ starting at $x_1 = X$ with integer widths $h_r = \lfloor x_r^\theta \rfloor$ and $x_{r+1} = x_r + h_r$, stopping at the last complete window inside $[X, AX]$. Write the logarithmic-integral expectation and normalized prime-count fluctuation on the $r$-th window as $$E_r := \int_{x_r}^{x_r + h_r} \frac{dt}{\ln t}, \qquad W_r := \frac{\pi(x_r + h_r - 1) - \pi(x_r - 1) - E_r}{\sqrt{2 h_r}}.$$ Heuristics combining the local variance of primes in short intervals [1, 4] with Gaussian tail asymptotics suggest a clean scale-dependent envelope with no remaining logarithmic factor: $$\lim_{X \to \infty} \max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} W_r = +(1 - \theta), \qquad \lim_{X \to \infty} \min_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} W_r = -(1 - \theta).$$ At Legendre's scale $\theta = 1/2$, consecutive square intervals $(n^2, (n+1)^2)$ have width $2n \sim 2\sqrt{x}$, so setting $c_n := \pi((n+1)^2 - 1) - \pi(n^2)$, $E_n := \int_{n^2}^{(n+1)^2} dt/\ln t$, and $D_n := (E_n - c_n)/\sqrt{n}$ translates the $\theta = 1/2$ law into $$\limsup_{n \to \infty} D_n = +1, \qquad \liminf_{n \to \infty} D_n = -1.$$ A closely related universal inequality $|c_n - n/\ln n| < \sqrt{n}$ for all $n > 1$ was conjectured by Rocchelli in September 2023 and checked through $n = 10^6$ by Pfoertner [8], and verified in a companion primesieve computation by the author through $n = 2{,}000{,}000$ with zero violations (maximum shortfall $0.941804\sqrt{n}$ at $n = 2{,}456$; maximum surplus $0.933150\sqrt{n}$ at $n = 3{,}516$).

In parallel, Montgomery and Soundararajan [1] conjectured that centered short-interval counts have asymptotically Gaussian central moments of every fixed order $K \ge 1$ when $(\ln N)^{1+\delta} \le H \le N^{1-\delta}$, and Leung [2] proved under the Riemann hypothesis (RH) and the linear independence of positive zeta-zero ordinates (LI) that finite collections of short intervals obey a multivariate Gaussian limit with weak negative neighbour correlations ($\approx -0.056$ to $-0.062$ at lag $1$ in tested square-gap and power-window bands). Because neighbouring windows are not independent and fixed-order Gaussian convergence controls only bounded standardized heights, two foundational questions arise:

  1. Does the short-interval extreme law follow from Montgomery and Soundararajan's moment conjectures, and if not at fixed order, up to what growing moment order must single-window moments hold?
  2. Why do empirical prime moments break away from Gaussian double-factorial scaling above a critical order, and why has $|D_n| < 1$ suffered zero violations across two million consecutive square gaps if $\limsup |D_n| = 1$?

This paper resolves these questions in four steps:

  • Section 2 (Proposition 1) proves two unconditional repairs required before the $\pm(1-\theta)$ extreme statement is well-posed: un-truncated cumulative maximization over $2 \le x_r \le X$ fails at $\theta = 0.85$ because the early window $[5, 8)$ permanently pins the maximum above $1 - \theta$, and replacing $E_r$ by the midpoint approximation $h_r/\ln(x_r + h_r/2)$ introduces an analytic drift $\sim x_r^{\frac52\theta - 2}/(24\sqrt{2}\ln^2 x_r)$ that diverges to $+\infty$ for all $\theta \in (4/5, 1)$. Prime powers $p^m$ ($m \ge 2$), by contrast, are negligible across all $0 < \theta < 1$.
  • Section 3 (Theorem 1) proves that neither the upper bound $\limsup W_r \le 1 - \theta$ nor the lower bound $\liminf \max W_r \ge 1 - \theta$ follows from Montgomery and Soundararajan's published fixed-order Conjecture 1 [1] — nor from any sub-logarithmic schedule $K_{\max}(X) = o(\ln X/\ln\ln X)$ even when all fixed-lag joint Gaussian moments hold — and derives the exact spectral curves of both countermodels. In sharp contrast, on macroscopic void scales, Conjecture 1 at the single fixed even order $K_\theta = 2\lfloor(1-\theta)/\theta\rfloor + 2$ rules out prime-free intervals $[x, x+x^\theta]$ for all large $x$: at $\theta = 1/2$, Selberg's second moment $K = 2$ [5, 6] sits on a logarithmic knife-edge [9, 10], whereas $K = 4$ alone implies eventual Legendre's conjecture.
  • Section 4 (Proposition 2 and Theorem 2) explains why $h = x^\theta$ lies in a sub-Cramér cumulant regime (unlike Granville and Lumley's logarithmic windows $h = (\ln x)^\alpha$ [3]), proves via Mehler's Hermite expansion that weak negative correlations [2] leave open-strip empirical moments exponentially concentrated, and proves deterministically — with zero independence assumption — that single-window moments on the open sub-critical strip $0 < \beta = m/((1-\theta)\ln X) < 1$ (Hypothesis UMS$0(\theta)$) imply both $\pm(1-\theta)$ limits and force an Ehrenfest second-order moment condensation transition at $K{\mathrm{crit}}(X) = 2(1-\theta)\ln X$ onto the universal curve $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta) = \beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1 - 1/\beta)}$ ($\beta > 1$). A zero-parameter finite-$M$ incomplete-gamma refinement $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{IG}}(m; M)$ matches actual prime moments up to $2m = 60$ within $0.0081$ at $\theta = 0.30$ ($574{,}918$ windows) and within $0.0051$ at Legendre's scale $\theta = 0.50$ ($14{,}143$ windows).
  • Section 5 (Theorem 3) shows why $2m$-th roots fix $\limsup |W_r| = 1 - \theta$ regardless of polynomial moment prefactors while leaving strict constant-$1$ cutoffs sensitive to secondary variance terms, and proves that Montgomery and Soundararajan's exact secondary constant $C_{\mathrm{sq}} = \gamma + \ln(4\pi) - 1 = 2.108240\dots$ shifts the expected $1.0$-crossing horizon on consecutive squares by 52.5 orders of magnitude relative to the naive one-term Gaussian variance (from $N \approx 10^{4.15}$ to $N \approx 10^{56.64}$ two-sided and $N \approx 10^{155.53}$ one-sided), predicting only $0.04706$ shortfalls below $n = 2{,}000{,}000$.

What this paper claims and what remains conditional. Propositions 1 and 2 and Theorems 1, 2, and 3 are unconditional mathematical theorems about finite-window inequalities, moment implications, and correlated Gaussian arrays. Applying Theorem 2 to actual primes requires Hypothesis UMS$_0(\theta)$ (uniform sub-critical Montgomery--Soundararajan moments on $0 < \beta < 1$), which is unproved for primes; Legendre's conjecture (verified unconditionally up to $n \le 7\times 10^{13}$ by Sorenson and Webster [11]) is likewise not proved unconditionally here. Spatial correlations between rare prime-window extremes — including universal $1/4$ adjacent-pair repulsion, $k^{-k}$ consecutive cluster suppression, and $4\times$ opposite-sign prime dipoles — are developed separately in our companion paper UOQR.

2. Exact Setup and Unconditional Boundary Repairs

For $0 < \theta < 1$ and $A > 1$ (we take $A = 4$ or $A = 2$ in numerical bands), the disjoint tiling $\mathcal{G}(X)$ of $[X, AX]$ has cardinality $$M(X) := |\mathcal{G}(X)| = \int_X^{AX} \frac{dt}{t^\theta}\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr) = \frac{A^{1-\theta} - 1}{1 - \theta},X^{1-\theta}\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr),$$ so its logarithmic sample size is $$L(X) := \ln M(X) = (1 - \theta)\ln X + \ln!\left(\frac{A^{1-\theta} - 1}{1 - \theta}\right) + o(1).$$ Before testing extreme limits against moment hypotheses, Proposition 1 establishes three unconditional facts that fix the domain and centering of $W_r$.

Proposition 1 (Unconditional Boundary, Centering, and Prime-Power Repairs). Fix $0 < \theta < 1$.

  1. (Failure of un-truncated maximization from $x_1 = 2$.) Let $\mathcal{G}{\mathrm{all}}(X)$ be the greedy disjoint tiling of $[2, X]$ starting at $x_1 = 2$ with $h_r = \max(1, \lfloor x_r^\theta \rfloor)$. At $\theta = 0.85$ ($1 - \theta = 0.1500$), the first three windows are $[2, 3)$, $[3, 5)$, and $[5, 8)$ ($x_3 = 5$, $h_3 = \lfloor 5^{0.85}\rfloor = 3$). Because $[5, 8)$ contains the two primes ${5, 7}$ while $E_3 = \int_5^8 dt/\ln t = 1.619090\dots$, $$W_3 = \frac{2 - 1.619090\dots}{\sqrt{6}} = 0.155505\dots > 0.1500 = 1 - \theta.$$ Hence $\max{r \in \mathcal{G}{\mathrm{all}}(X)} W_r \ge 0.155505 > 1 - \theta$ for every $X \ge 8$. Any valid statement of the $(1-\theta)$ extreme law must therefore let the lower cutoff tend to infinity (either via $\limsup{r\to\infty} W_r$ or on a moving band $\mathcal{G}(X) \subset [X, AX]$ as $X \to \infty$).
  2. (Divergence of midpoint centering for $\theta > 4/5$.) Let $E_r^{\mathrm{mid}} := \frac{h_r}{\ln(x_r + h_r/2)}$ and $W_r^{\mathrm{mid}} := (\pi(x_r + h_r - 1) - \pi(x_r - 1) - E_r^{\mathrm{mid}})/\sqrt{2h_r}$. Since $f(t) = (\ln t)^{-1}$ satisfies $f''(t) = \frac{\ln t + 2}{t^2(\ln t)^3} > 0$ for all $t > 1$, midpoint quadrature strictly underestimates $E_r$ on every window, creating a deterministic positive shift $$W_r^{\mathrm{mid}} - W_r = \frac{E_r - E_r^{\mathrm{mid}}}{\sqrt{2 h_r}} = \frac{h_r^{5/2}}{24\sqrt{2}},\frac{\ln \xi_r + 2}{\xi_r^2(\ln \xi_r)^3} \sim \frac{x_r^{\frac52\theta - 2}}{24\sqrt{2},(\ln x_r)^2}$$ for some $\xi_r \in (x_r, x_r + h_r)$. This bias tends to $0$ when $0 < \theta \le 4/5$, but diverges to $+\infty$ for every $\theta \in (4/5, 1)$ (and for left-endpoint centering $h_r/\ln x_r$, diverges already for $\theta > 2/3$).
  3. (Uniform negligibility of higher prime powers across $0 < \theta < 1$.) Writing $\psi(x_r + h_r - 1) - \psi(x_r - 1) = \sum_{x_r \le p < x_r + h_r}\ln p + R_{\mathrm{pp}}(r)$, the prime-power remainder $R_{\mathrm{pp}}(r) = \sum_{m \ge 2}\sum_{x_r \le p^m < x_r + h_r}\ln p$ satisfies $$\frac{R_{\mathrm{pp}}(r)}{\sqrt{2 h_r},\ln x_r} = O!\left(\frac{x_r^{-(1-\theta)/2}}{\ln x_r} + \frac{\ln x_r}{x_r^{\theta/2}}\right) \xrightarrow{x_r \to \infty} 0$$ uniformly on $\mathcal{G}(X)$ for every fixed $\theta \in (0, 1)$.

Proof. Part (1) is a direct evaluation: $5^{0.85} = 3.9267\dots$ gives $h_3 = 3$, and numerical quadrature gives $\int_5^8 dt/\ln t = 1.61909039\dots$, whence $(2 - E_3)/\sqrt{6} = 0.1555056\dots > 0.15$. For part (2), Taylor expansion of $f(t) = (\ln t)^{-1}$ about the midpoint $m_r = x_r + h_r/2$ integrates the linear term to zero and yields $E_r - h_r f(m_r) = \int_{-h_r/2}^{h_r/2} \frac12 u^2 f''(\eta_r(u)),du = \frac{h_r^3}{24} f''(\xi_r)$ by the weighted mean-value theorem. Differentiating $f'(t) = -t^{-1}(\ln t)^{-2}$ gives $f''(t) = t^{-2}(\ln t)^{-2} + 2t^{-2}(\ln t)^{-3} = (\ln t + 2)/(t^2\ln^3 t)$. Substituting $h_r = x_r^\theta + O(1)$ and $\xi_r \sim x_r$ gives $h_r^{5/2}/(24\sqrt{2},x_r^2\ln^2 x_r) = x_r^{\frac52\theta - 2}/(24\sqrt{2}\ln^2 x_r)$, whose exponent $\frac52\theta - 2$ is positive if and only if $\theta > 4/5$. (For $h_r/\ln x_r$, the error is $\frac12 h_r^2 |f'(\xi_r)|/\sqrt{2h_r} \asymp x_r^{\frac32\theta - 1}/\ln^2 x_r$, diverging for $\theta > 2/3$.) For part (3), each $m \ge 3$ contributes $O(x_r^{\theta - 2/3} + \ln x_r)$ across $O(\ln x_r)$ exponents, totaling $O(x_r^{\theta - 2/3}\ln x_r + \ln^2 x_r)$. For $m = 2$, the interval $[\sqrt{x_r}, \sqrt{x_r + h_r})$ has length $\asymp h_r/\sqrt{x_r} \asymp x_r^{\theta - 1/2}$: if $\theta \le 1/2$ it contains $O(1)$ integers, contributing $O(\ln x_r)$; if $\theta > 1/2$, the Brun--Titchmarsh inequality bounds the prime count in $[\sqrt{x_r}, \sqrt{x_r + h_r})$ by $O(x_r^{\theta - 1/2}/\ln x_r)$, contributing $O(x_r^{\theta - 1/2})$ to $R_{\mathrm{pp}}(r)$. Dividing by $\sqrt{2h_r}\ln x_r \asymp x_r^{\theta/2}\ln x_r$ yields $O(x_r^{-(1-\theta)/2}/\ln x_r + x_r^{-\theta/2}\ln x_r) \to 0$. $\square$

3. Undecidability from Fixed Moments vs. Eventual Legendre at $K = 4$

In [1, p. 594], Montgomery and Soundararajan state the following conjecture for the moments of $\psi(n + H) - \psi(n) - H$:

  • Conjecture 1 of Montgomery--Soundararajan (fixed $K$). For each fixed integer $K \ge 1$, uniformly for $(\ln N)^{1+\delta} \le H \le N^{1-\delta}$, $$M_K(N; H) := \sum_{n=1}^N \bigl(\psi(n+H) - \psi(n) - H\bigr)^K = \bigl(\mu_K + o_K(1)\bigr),N,\bigl(H\ln(N/H)\bigr)^{K/2},$$ where $\mu_K = (K - 1)!!$ for even $K$ and $\mu_K = 0$ for odd $K$.

(Their conditional Theorem 3 in [1] establishes this asymptotic under a strong uniform Hardy--Littlewood $k$-tuple error bound $O(N^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ only in the restricted range $H \le N^{1/K}$, which at $H = N^\theta$ caps $K \le \lfloor 1/\theta \rfloor$.)

Theorem 1 proves that fixed-order or sub-logarithmic moments leave $\sqrt{2h}$ extremes completely undetermined in both directions, yet already settle macroscopic prime existence — including eventual Legendre's conjecture at the single fixed moment $K = 4$.

Theorem 1 (What Fixed and Sub-Logarithmic Moments Do and Do Not Imply). Fix $0 < \theta < 1$.

  1. (Upper-bound undecidability and first-order spectral breakout.) Let $K_{\max}(X) \ge 1$ satisfy $K_{\max}(X) = o(\ln X / \ln\ln X)$, and let $\Lambda \in (1 - \theta, \infty)$ be any prescribed constant. There exists a binary sequence $I_n \in {0, 1}$ ($n \ge 2$) of local density $\sim 1/\ln n$ whose window counts $C(x, h) = \sum_{x \le n < x+h} I_n$ satisfy both the all-shifts moment asymptotic $\frac{1}{X}\sum_{n=X}^{2X}(C(n, H) - E(n, H))^K / (H(1-\theta)/\ln X)^{K/2} = \mu_K + o(1)$ ($H = \lfloor X^\theta \rfloor$) and the disjoint-tiling moment asymptotic $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r^K = \mu_K + o(1)$ ($Z_r := \sqrt{2\ln X/(1-\theta)},W_r$) uniformly for all $1 \le K \le K_{\max}(X)$ — together with all fixed-order, fixed-lag joint Gaussian moments with Coulomb correlation $-\Delta(d)/((1-\theta)\ln X)$ — while satisfying $$\lim_{X \to \infty} \max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} W_r = +\Lambda \qquad \text{and} \qquad \lim_{X \to \infty} \min_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} W_r = -\Lambda.$$ Moreover, writing $\Lambda_\star := \Lambda / (1 - \theta) > 1$ and $\beta = m/\ln M$, its standardized $2m$-th root ratio converges for every $\beta > 0$ to $$\Phi_{\mathrm{spike}}(\beta; \Lambda_\star) = \max!\left(1,; \Lambda_\star,\frac{e^{\frac12(1 - 1/\beta)}}{\sqrt{\beta}}\right),$$ which breaks out of the Gaussian plateau $\Phi = 1$ with a first-order slope discontinuity at the unique root $\beta_{\mathrm{break}}(\Lambda_\star) \in (0, 1)$ of $\beta e^{1/\beta - 1} = \Lambda_\star^2$.
  2. (Eventual Legendre's conjecture from the single fixed moment $K = 4$.) Conversely, assume Montgomery and Soundararajan's Conjecture 1 holds solely at the single fixed even integer $$K_\theta := 2\left\lfloor \frac{1 - \theta}{\theta}\right\rfloor + 2.$$ Then for every fixed $\delta \in (0, 1]$ and all sufficiently large $x$, every interval $[x, x + x^\theta]$ contains at least $(1 - \delta)x^\theta / \ln x$ primes. In particular, at $\theta = 1/2$, Conjecture 1 at the single fixed order $K = 4$ implies Legendre's conjecture for all sufficiently large $n$.
  3. (Lower-bound undecidability and premature condensation.) Fix $\alpha \in (0, 1)$. Cycling through $\lfloor M^\alpha \rfloor$ realizations of a stationary Gaussian sequence (independent or MA-correlated with lag-$1$ correlation $-\ln 2/((1-\theta)\ln X)$) across the $M$ windows of $\mathcal{G}(X)$ satisfies all single-window and fixed-lag Gaussian moment asymptotics up to $K \le 2\beta_0(1-\theta)\ln X$ for every $\beta_0 < \alpha$, condenses prematurely at $\beta = \alpha < 1$ onto $$\Phi_{\mathrm{rec}}(\beta; \alpha) = \begin{cases} 1, & 0 < \beta \le \alpha, \ \sqrt{\alpha/\beta};e^{\frac12(1 - \alpha/\beta)}, & \beta > \alpha, \end{cases}$$ and collapses the extreme limits to $\lim \max W_r = +\sqrt{\alpha}(1-\theta)$ and $\lim \min W_r = -\sqrt{\alpha}(1-\theta)$ in probability (and almost surely along dyadic bands $X_j = 2^j$).

Proof. For (1), let $(G_n){n\ge 2}$ be a stationary Gaussian sequence whose block sums reproduce the local covariance structure, and threshold $I_n^{(0)} := \mathbf{1}{G_n > \Phi^{-1}(1 - 1/\ln n)}$. By Mehler's Hermite expansion (Proposition 2(2) below) and Pickands' extreme theorem [7], $I_n^{(0)}$ satisfies the stated bulk moments and $\max_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X_j)} |W_r^{(0)}| = (1 - \theta)(1 + o(1))$ almost surely across $X_j = 2^j$; fix one deterministic realization. In each dyadic band $[X_j, 2X_j]$, select two interior windows $I_{j,+}, I_{j,-} \in \mathcal{G}(X_j)$ separated by at least $2H_j$ ($H_j = \lfloor X_j^\theta \rfloor$). Since $\Lambda\sqrt{2H_j} = o(H_j/\ln X_j)$ while $I_{j,\pm}$ each contain $\sim H_j/\ln X_j$ ones and $\sim H_j$ zeros, we may flip integers inside the middle half of $I_{j,+}$ and $I_{j,-}$ so that $W_{r_+} = \Lambda + O(H_j^{-1/2})$ and $W_{r_-} = -\Lambda + O(H_j^{-1/2})$ ($Z_{r_\pm} = \pm\Lambda_\star\sqrt{2L_j}(1 + o(1))$). At most $4H_j$ shifts $n \in [X_j, 2X_j]$ intersect $I_{j,+}\cup I_{j,-}$, and on each such shift $|Z(n)| \le C\Lambda_\star\sqrt{\ln X_j}$. The resulting perturbation to $\frac{1}{X_j}\sum_{n=X_j}^{2X_j} Z(n)^K$ (and similarly to $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X_j)} Z_r^K$) is bounded by $$O!\left(\frac{H_j}{X_j}\right)\bigl(C\Lambda_\star\sqrt{\ln X_j}\bigr)^K = \exp!\Bigl(-(1-\theta)\ln X_j + \tfrac12 K\ln\ln X_j + O(K)\Bigr) \xrightarrow{j\to\infty} 0$$ uniformly for $1 \le K \le K_{\max}(X_j)$ whenever $K_{\max}(X)\ln\ln X = o(\ln X)$. At growing even order $2m = 2\lfloor \beta L_j\rfloor$, write $\sum_{r=1}^M Z_r^{2m} = \sum_{r \notin {r_+, r_-}} (Z_r^{(0)})^{2m} + 2\Lambda_\star^{2m}(2L_j)^m(1 + o(1))^{2m}$. Dividing by $M = e^{L_j + O(1)}$, taking $2m$-th roots, and dividing by $\mu_{2m}^{1/(2m)} \sim \sqrt{2\beta L_j / e}$ gives the maximum of the bulk ratio $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta) \le 1$ and the spike ratio $e^{-1/(2\beta)}\Lambda_\star\sqrt{2L_j}/\sqrt{2\beta L_j/e} = \Lambda_\star\beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1-1/\beta)}$. Since $\beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1-1/\beta)}$ increases strictly from $0$ to $1$ on $(0, 1]$, the spike overtakes $1$ precisely above $\beta_{\mathrm{break}}(\Lambda_\star) \in (0, 1)$, where $\frac{d}{d\beta}\bigl(\Lambda_\star\beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1-1/\beta)}\bigr)\big|{\beta{\mathrm{break}}} = \frac{1 - \beta_{\mathrm{break}}}{2\beta_{\mathrm{break}}^2} > 0$.

For (2), suppose an interval $[x_\star, x_\star + x_\star^\theta]$ with $x_\star \in [N/2, N]$ contains at most $(1 - \delta)x_\star^\theta/\ln x_\star$ primes ($\delta \in (0, 1]$; the case $\delta = 1$ is a prime-free interval). Set $\varepsilon_\delta := \frac{\theta\delta}{4} > 0$ and $H_2 := \lfloor (1 + \varepsilon_\delta)x_\star^\theta\rfloor \asymp_{\theta,\delta} N^\theta$. For every integer shift $n \in [x_\star - \varepsilon_\delta x_\star^\theta, x_\star]$, the shifted window $[n, n + H_2]$ covers $[x_\star, x_\star + x_\star^\theta]$ together with at most $\varepsilon_\delta x_\star^\theta + 1$ exterior integers. Including higher prime powers on $[x_\star, x_\star + x_\star^\theta]$ adds $O(x_\star^{\theta - 1/2} + \ln^2 x_\star) = o(x_\star^\theta)$ to $\psi$, while by the Brun--Titchmarsh inequality an exterior block of length $y = \varepsilon_\delta x_\star^\theta$ contains at most $\frac{2y}{\ln y}(1 + o(1)) = \frac{2\varepsilon_\delta}{\theta}\frac{x_\star^\theta}{\ln x_\star}(1 + o(1)) = \frac{\delta}{2}\frac{x_\star^\theta}{\ln x_\star}(1 + o(1))$ primes and hence contributes at most $(\frac{\delta}{2} + o(1))x_\star^\theta$ to $\psi$. Consequently, for all $\lfloor \varepsilon_\delta x_\star^\theta \rfloor \gg_{\theta,\delta} N^\theta$ shifts $n$ in that range, $$\psi(n + H_2) - \psi(n) ;\le; \bigl(1 - \tfrac{\delta}{2} + o(1)\bigr)x_\star^\theta ;=; H_2 - \bigl(\tfrac{\delta}{2} + \varepsilon_\delta - o(1)\bigr)x_\star^\theta,$$ so $|\psi(n + H_2) - \psi(n) - H_2| \ge \frac{\delta}{2}x_\star^\theta$. Raising to the even power $K = K_\theta$ and summing over those shifts yields $$M_{K_\theta}(2N; H_2) ;\gg_{\theta,\delta}; N^\theta \cdot (N^\theta)^{K_\theta} = N^{\theta(K_\theta + 1)}.$$ Under Conjecture 1 at fixed even order $K = K_\theta$, $M_{K_\theta}(2N; H_2) \ll N(H_2\ln N)^{K_\theta/2} \asymp_{\theta,\delta} N^{1 + \theta K_\theta/2}(\ln N)^{K_\theta/2}$. By choice of $K_\theta = 2\lfloor\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\rfloor + 2 > \frac{2(1-\theta)}{\theta}$, the exponent $\theta(K_\theta + 1)$ strictly exceeds $1 + \frac12\theta K_\theta$, a contradiction for all large $N$. At $\theta = 1/2$, $K_{1/2} = 4$ gives $N^{5/2}$ against $N^2(\ln N)^2$; taking $x_\star = n^2$ and $\delta = 1/2$ certifies at least $(\frac12 - o(1))n/\ln n$ primes in $(n^2, (n+1)^2)$ for all sufficiently large $n$.

Part (3) follows by applying Theorem 2(b) below to the empirical measure of $M_{\mathrm{sub}} = \lfloor M^\alpha \rfloor$ Gaussian variables (whose own logarithmic size is $L_{\mathrm{sub}} = \alpha L + O(1)$, so rescaling $\beta_{\mathrm{sub}} = m/(\alpha L) = \beta/\alpha$ substitutes $\beta \mapsto \beta/\alpha$ into $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}$). $\square$

Remark (Why Selberg's $K = 2$ barely misses Legendre while $K = 4$ succeeds). At $\theta = 1/2$, setting $K = 2$ gives $\theta(K + 1) = \frac32 = 1 + \frac12\theta K$: the void lower bound $N^{3/2}$ falls below Selberg's RH bound $N^{3/2}(\ln N)^2$ [5, 6] by $(\ln N)^2$, leaving room for $O(\ln^2 M)$ exceptional square intervals per dyadic band ($O(\ln^3 M)$ below $M^2$, refined to $O(f(M^2)\ln^2 M)$ by Bazzanella [9]; compare Proposition 3 of [10]). Stepping to the single fixed moment $K = 4$ breaks the exponent tie by $N^{1/2}/(\ln N)^2$ and eliminates all large exceptions.

4. Moment Condensation and Deterministic Proof of the Extreme Law

Standardize each window on $\mathcal{G}(X)$ to asymptotic unit variance by setting $$Z_r := \sqrt{\frac{2\ln X}{1 - \theta}},W_r \qquad (r \in \mathcal{G}(X),; |\mathcal{G}(X)| = M = e^{L(X)}),$$ so that $\max_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X)} W_r \to +(1 - \theta)$ and $\min_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X)} W_r \to -(1 - \theta)$ are equivalent to $\max_r Z_r/\sqrt{2L} \to +1$ and $\min_r Z_r/\sqrt{2L} \to -1$.

4.1 Sub-Cramér Cumulants and Open-Strip Concentration

Before stating the deterministic moment theorem, Proposition 2 explains two structural facts: (a) why polynomial windows $h = x^\theta$ retain the Gaussian extreme constant $1 - \theta$ rather than deforming as logarithmic windows $h = (\ln x)^\alpha$ do [3], and (b) why weak negative correlations between neighbouring windows [2] do not disturb empirical moments on the open sub-critical strip $0 < \beta < 1$.

Proposition 2 (Sub-Cramér Cumulant Decay and Mehler--Hermite Concentration).

  1. (Sub-Cramér saddlepoint scaling for $h = x^\theta$.) Let $Y$ be a centered random variable with mean parameter $\lambda = X^\theta/\ln X$, variance $\sigma^2 \sim (1-\theta)\lambda$, and cumulants satisfying linear sieve-type bounds $|\kappa_r(Y)| \le C^r r!,\lambda$ for all $r \ge 3$. At the extreme deviation height $y_\star = (1-\theta)\sqrt{2X^\theta}$, the saddlepoint tilt $t_\star := y_\star/\sigma^2 = \sqrt{2},X^{-\theta/2}\ln X \bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr)$ tends to $0$, and all non-Gaussian cumulants in the Cramér series satisfy $$\sum_{r=3}^{\infty} \frac{|\kappa_r(Y)|}{r!},t_\star^r ;\ll; \lambda t_\star^3 ;\asymp; \frac{(\ln X)^2}{X^{\theta/2}} \xrightarrow{X \to \infty} 0 \qquad (\forall,\theta \in (0, 1)).$$ By contrast, on logarithmic windows $h = (\ln X)^\alpha$, $\lambda t_\star^3 \asymp (\ln X)^{2 - \alpha/2}$ fails to vanish for $\alpha \le 4$, altering the extreme constant [3].
  2. (Exponential concentration on $0 < \beta < 1$ under Coulomb covariance.) Let $(Z_1, \dots, Z_M) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathcal{C}X)$ ($M = e^{L}$, $L = (1-\theta)\ln X + O(1)$) have unit diagonal and Leung's short-interval off-diagonal correlations [2] $$\rho{jk} = -\frac{\Delta(|j - k|)}{(1 - \theta)\ln X + O(1)} < 0 \quad (j \ne k), \qquad \Delta(d) := \frac12\bigl((d+1)\ln(d+1) - 2d\ln d + (d-1)\ln(d-1)\bigr).$$ Then for every fixed $\beta \in (0, 1)$ and $m = \lfloor \beta L \rfloor$, as $X \to \infty$, $$\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^M Z_r^{2m} = \bigl(1 + o_{\mathbb{P}}(1)\bigr),\mu_{2m} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^M Z_r^{2m - 1}\right| = o_{\mathbb{P}}!\left(\frac{\mu_{2m}}{\sqrt{2m}}\right),$$ and uniformly for $1 \le m \le \beta L$, $\bigl(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^M Z_r^{2m}\bigr)^{1/(2m)} / \mu_{2m}^{1/(2m)} \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} 1$ (and almost surely along dyadic bands $X_j = 2^j$).

Proof. Part (1) is immediate on summing the geometric tail for $C t_\star \le \frac12$: $\sum_{r\ge 3} C^r \lambda t_\star^r \le 2C^3 \lambda t_\star^3 \asymp (X^\theta/\ln X)(X^{-3\theta/2}\ln^3 X) = X^{-\theta/2}(\ln X)^2 \to 0$. For part (2), truncate each coordinate at $B_L := \sqrt{2L + 2\ln L}$, setting $\widetilde{Z}r := Z_r\mathbf{1}{|Z_r|\le B_L}$. By a union bound, $\mathbb{P}(\exists r : |Z_r| > B_L) \le 2M\Phi(-B_L) \ll L^{-3/2} \to 0$ (summable over dyadic bands $L_j \asymp j\ln 2$). Fix $\beta \in (0, 1)$ and $m = \lfloor \beta L\rfloor$. To bound the diagonal fourth moment $\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}]$, consider two cases: if $\beta \le \frac12$, $\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}] \le \mu{4m} \ll 2^{2m}\mu{2m}^2$, so $\frac{1}{M}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}]/\mu{2m}^2 \ll e^{-(1 - 2\beta\ln 2)L} \le e^{-(1 - \ln 2)L} \to 0$; if $\beta \in (\frac12, 1)$, then $4m \ge 2L$ for large $L$, so $\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}] \le B_L^{4m - 2L}\mathbb{E}[Z_1^{2L}] \ll L^{O(1)}(2L)^{2m - L}(2L/e)^L = L^{O(1)}(2L)^{2m}e^{-L}$, whence $$\frac{\frac{1}{M}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}]}{\mu{2m}^2} ;\ll; L^{O(1)},\exp\bigl(-2(1 - \beta + \beta\ln\beta)L\bigr) \xrightarrow{L\to\infty} 0,$$ since $1 - \beta + \beta\ln\beta > 0$ for all $\beta \in (0, 1)$. For off-diagonal pairs $j \ne k$, expand the even function $g_m(z) := z^{2m}\mathbf{1}{|z|\le B_L}$ in probabilists' Hermite polynomials $g_m(z) = \sum_{q\ge 0} c_{m,q}\operatorname{He}{2q}(z)$. By Mehler's identity $\operatorname{Cov}(\operatorname{He}r(Z_j), \operatorname{He}s(Z_k)) = \delta{rs} r!,\rho{jk}^r$, Parseval's identity $\sum{q\ge 1}(2q)!,c_{m,q}^2 \le \mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}]$, and $\Delta(d) = \frac{1}{2d} + O(d^{-3})$ (which gives $\sum{d=1}^M \rho_d^2 \ll (\ln X)^{-2}\sum_{d=1}^\infty d^{-2} = O((\ln X)^{-2})$), we obtain $$\left|\frac{1}{M^2}\sum_{j \ne k} \operatorname{Cov}\bigl(\widetilde{Z}j^{2m}, \widetilde{Z}k^{2m}\bigr)\right| ;\le; \frac{2}{M}\left(\sum{d=1}^M \rho_d^2\right)\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}] ;\ll; \frac{\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m}]}{M(\ln X)^2}.$$ Moreover, the truncation bias satisfies $\mu{2m} - \mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{2m}] = \int{|z|>B_L} z^{2m}\phi(z),dz \ll B_L^{2m-1}e^{-B_L^2/2} \ll L^{-3/2}e^{-(1-\beta+\beta\ln\beta)L}\mu{2m}$. For odd order $2m - 1$, $\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{2m-1}] = 0$ by symmetry, and the same Hermite--Parseval bound on $h_m(z) := z^{2m-1}\mathbf{1}{|z|\le B_L}$ (using $|\rho{jk}|^{2q+1} \le |\rho_{jk}|$ and $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{d=1}^M |\rho_d| \ll \frac{\ln M}{M\ln X} = O(M^{-1})$) bounds $\operatorname{Var}(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^M \widetilde{Z}_r^{2m-1})$ by $O(M^{-1}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}1^{4m-2}]) = o((\mu{2m}/\sqrt{2m})^2)$. Chebyshev's inequality (and a union bound over the $\le L$ integers $1 \le m \le \beta L$ for $2m$-th roots) completes the proof. $\square$

4.2 Deterministic Sub-Critical Moment Proof and Ehrenfest Condensation

Motivated by Proposition 2(2), we formulate our conditional input solely on the open sub-critical strip $0 < \beta < 1$, strictly below the condensation point $\beta = 1$:

  • Hypothesis $\mathrm{UMS}_0(\theta)$ (Open-Strip Uniform Sub-Critical Moments). With $L = L(X) = \ln|\mathcal{G}(X)|$ and $Z_r = \sqrt{\frac{2\ln X}{1-\theta}},W_r$:
    1. (UMS$_0$-even): For every fixed $\beta_0 \in (0, 1)$, uniformly across $1 \le m \le \beta_0 L$, $\bigl(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r^{2m}\bigr)^{1/(2m)} = (1 + o(1))\mu_{2m}^{1/(2m)}$, and at each fixed $\beta \in (0, 1)$ with $m = \lfloor\beta L\rfloor$, $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r^{2m} = (1 + o(1))\mu_{2m}$.
    2. (UMS$_0$-odd): For each fixed $\beta \in (0, 1)$ and $m = \lfloor\beta L\rfloor$, $\bigl|\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r^{2m-1}\bigr| = o\bigl(\mu_{2m}/\sqrt{2m}\bigr)$ as $X \to \infty$.

Theorem 2 (Deterministic Equivalence of Open-Strip Moments, Short-Interval Extremes, and Second-Order Condensation). Fix $0 < \theta < 1$, and let $\mathcal{G}(X)$ be the disjoint tiling of $[X, AX]$ ($A > 1$).

  1. (Unsigned extreme limit from open-strip even moments alone.) Under (UMS$_0$-even) alone — with no assumption on the boundary moment $\beta = 1$, on odd moments, or on independence or correlations between windows — $$\lim_{X \to \infty} \max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} |W_r| = 1 - \theta.$$
  2. (Universal Ehrenfest second-order condensation across all $\beta > 0$.) Under (UMS$_0$-even) alone, for every real $\beta > 0$ (including $\beta = 1$ and the entire super-critical phase $\beta > 1$), $$\lim_{X \to \infty} \frac{\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r^{2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor}\right)^{!1/(2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor)}}{\mu_{2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor}^{1/(2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor)}} = \Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta) := \begin{cases} 1, & 0 < \beta \le 1, \ \dfrac{e^{\frac12(1 - 1/\beta)}}{\sqrt{\beta}}, & \beta > 1. \end{cases}$$ Here $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta) < 1$ for all $\beta > 1$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(1) = 1$, $\Phi'{\mathrm{cond}}(1^-) = \Phi'{\mathrm{cond}}(1^+) = 0$, and $\Phi''{\mathrm{cond}}(1^+) - \Phi''{\mathrm{cond}}(1^-) = -\frac12$ (a second-order freezing transition analogous to random-energy-model condensation [12]). In particular, Montgomery and Soundararajan's Gaussian moment formula cannot hold for $K > K_{\mathrm{crit}}(X) = 2(1 - \theta)\ln X$.
  3. (Both signed extreme limits via Hölder separation.) Under (UMS$_0$-even) and (UMS$_0$-odd) — again with no assumption on independence or joint distributions — $$\lim_{X \to \infty} \max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} W_r = +(1 - \theta) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \lim_{X \to \infty} \min_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} W_r = -(1 - \theta).$$
  4. (Grid-phase transfer from all-shifts moments.) Partitioning ${X, \dots, X + MH - 1}$ ($H = \lfloor X^\theta \rfloor$) into the $H$ shifted tilings $\mathcal{G}a(X)$ ($0 \le a < H$) gives $\frac{1}{MH}\sum{n=X}^{X+MH-1} Z(n)^{2m} = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{a=0}^{H-1}\bigl(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{G}a(X)} Z{r,a}^{2m}\bigr)$. Hence the all-shifts upper bound $M_{2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor}(X; H) \le X(1 + o(1))^{2\beta L}\mu_{2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor}(H\ln(X/H))^{\lfloor\beta L\rfloor}$ across $\beta \in (0, 1)$ forces $\limsup_{X\to\infty}\max_{r\in\mathcal{G}a(X)} |W{r,a}| \le 1 - \theta$ simultaneously on a $1 - o(1)$ fraction of all grid phases $a$.

Proof. We prove the four parts in five steps.

Step 1 (Upper bound in (1) by letting $\beta \uparrow 1^-$). Fix any $\beta \in (0, 1)$ and set $m = \lfloor \beta L\rfloor$. For every $r_0 \in \mathcal{G}(X)$, $|Z_{r_0}|^{2m} \le \sum_{r=1}^M Z_r^{2m} = M(1 + o(1))^{2m}\mu_{2m}$. Since $M^{1/(2m)} = e^{\frac{1}{2\beta} + O(L^{-1})}$ and $\mu_{2m}^{1/(2m)} = \sqrt{2\beta L/e},(1 + O(L^{-1}))$, taking $2m$-th roots gives $$\limsup_{X \to \infty} \frac{\max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} |Z_r|}{\sqrt{2L}} ;\le; \sqrt{\beta},e^{\frac12(1/\beta - 1)}.$$ Letting $\beta \uparrow 1^-$ sends the right-hand side to $1$, proving $\limsup_{X\to\infty}\max_r |W_r| \le 1 - \theta$.

Step 2 (Lower bound in (1) via sub-critical Markov--layer-cake contradiction). Suppose along a sequence $X_j \to \infty$ that $\max_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X_j)} |Z_r| \le c\sqrt{2L}$ for some fixed $c \in (0, 1)$. Choose $\beta_1 := \frac{1 + c^2}{2} \in (c^2, 1)$ strictly inside the open sub-critical strip, and set $m_1 := \lfloor \beta_1 L\rfloor$. Let $\nu_M := \frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^M \delta_{Z_r}$, supported on $[-c\sqrt{2L}, c\sqrt{2L}]$. By (UMS$_0$-even) on the compact sub-strip $1 \le m \le \beta_1 L$, there exist $\varepsilon_j \to 0$ with $\int z^{2m},d\nu_M \le e^{\varepsilon_j L}\mu_{2m}$ for all $1 \le m \le \beta_1 L$. For every $u \in [L^{-1/2}, c]$, $m_u := \lfloor u^2 L\rfloor$ satisfies $1 \le m_u \le c^2 L < \beta_1 L$, so Markov's inequality gives $$\nu_M(|z| \ge u\sqrt{2L}) ;\le; \frac{\int z^{2m_u},d\nu_M}{(2u^2 L)^{m_u}} ;\le; e^{\varepsilon_j L}\frac{\mu_{2m_u}}{(2u^2 L)^{m_u}} ;\le; 2\sqrt{L},\exp\bigl(-u^2 L + \varepsilon_j L + 1\bigr),$$ using $\mu_{2m} \le 2\sqrt{m}(2m/e)^m$, $(m_u/(u^2 L))^{m_u} \le 1$, and $e^{-m_u} \le e^{1 - u^2 L}$. Substituting $y = u\sqrt{2L}$ into the layer-cake representation on $[0, c\sqrt{2L}]$ (and bounding $u \le L^{-1/2}$ by $1$) yields $$\int z^{2m_1},d\nu_M = 2m_1\int_0^{c\sqrt{2L}} y^{2m_1 - 1}\nu_M(|z| \ge y),dy ;\le; 1 + C L^{3/2}(2L)^{m_1} e^{\varepsilon_j L}\int_{L^{-1/2}}^c u^{2m_1 - 1} e^{-u^2 L},du.$$ Since $\frac{d}{dv}(v^{\beta_1} e^{-v}) = (\beta_1 - v)v^{\beta_1 - 1}e^{-v} > 0$ for $0 < v \le c^2 < \beta_1$, $\sup_{0 < u \le c} (u^2)^{\beta_1} e^{-u^2} = c^{2\beta_1} e^{-c^2} < \beta_1^{\beta_1} e^{-\beta_1}$. With $\eta_c := \beta_1\ln(\beta_1/c^2) - (\beta_1 - c^2) > 0$, we obtain $\int z^{2m_1},d\nu_M \ll L^2 e^{-(\eta_c - \varepsilon_j)L}\mu_{2m_1} = o(\mu_{2m_1})$, contradicting (UMS$_0$-even) at $\beta_1 \in (0, 1)$. Together with Step 1, this proves (1).

Step 3 (Exact super-critical condensation curve in (2)). For $0 < \beta < 1$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta) = 1$ holds by (UMS$_0$-even). Now let $\beta \ge 1$ and $m = \lfloor \beta L\rfloor$. By part (1), there exists $r_\star \in \mathcal{G}(X)$ with $|Z_{r_\star}| = (1 + o(1))\sqrt{2L}$, while $\max_r |Z_r| = (1 + o(1))\sqrt{2L}$. For the lower bound, keeping the single term $r = r_\star$ gives $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r=1}^M Z_r^{2m} \ge e^{-L + O(1)}\bigl((1+o(1))\sqrt{2L}\bigr)^{2m}$, whose $2m$-th root divided by $\mu_{2m}^{1/(2m)} \sim \sqrt{2\beta L/e}$ is $(1 + o(1))\beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1 - 1/\beta)}$. For the upper bound, fix any $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$ and split the layer-cake integral at $y_\varepsilon = (1 - \varepsilon)\sqrt{2L}$. On $[0, y_\varepsilon]$, the Markov estimate of Step 2 uses only $m_u \le (1-\varepsilon)^2 L < L$ and $\sup_{0\le v\le (1-\varepsilon)^2} v^\beta e^{-v} = (1-\varepsilon)^{2\beta}e^{-(1-\varepsilon)^2}$, giving $2m$-th root ratio at most $(1-\varepsilon)\beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1 - (1-\varepsilon)^2/\beta)}(1 + o(1))$. On $[y_\varepsilon, \max_r |Z_r|]$, monotonicity gives $\nu_M(|z|\ge y) \le \nu_M(|z|\ge y_\varepsilon) \le 2\sqrt{L},e^{-(1-\varepsilon)^2 L + o(L)}$, contributing at most $(\max_r |Z_r|)^{2m}\cdot 2\sqrt{L},e^{-(1-\varepsilon)^2 L + o(L)}$, whose $2m$-th root ratio is $\beta^{-1/2}e^{\frac12(1 - (1-\varepsilon)^2/\beta)}(1 + o(1))$. Sending $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ matches the lower bound. Writing $\psi(\beta) = \ln\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta) = \frac12(1 - \beta^{-1} - \ln\beta)$ on $\beta \ge 1$ gives $\psi'(1^+) = 0$ and $\psi''(1^+) = -\frac12$, establishing the second-order jump.

Step 4 (Signed lower bounds in (3) via $(3/2, 3)$-Hölder separation). Suppose $\max_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X_j)} Z_r \le c\sqrt{2L}$ with $c \in (0, 1)$ (the case $\min_r Z_r \ge -c\sqrt{2L}$ follows upon replacing $Z_r$ by $-Z_r$). Set $\beta := \frac{1 + c^2}{2} \in (c^2, 1)$, $m := \lfloor \beta L\rfloor$, and $Z_r = Z_r^+ - Z_r^-$ with $Z_r^\pm = \max(\pm Z_r, 0)$. Since $Z_r^+ \le c\sqrt{2L}$, the layer-cake estimate of Step 2 gives $\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^+)^{2m}] = o(\mu{2m})$ and $\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^+)^{2m-1}] = o(\mu{2m}/\sqrt{2m})$. By (UMS$_0$-even) at $\beta \in (0, 1)$ (note $\lfloor \beta L\rfloor + 1$ has the same asymptotic parameter $\beta \in (0, 1)$, so $\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[Z^{2m+2}] = (1 + o(1))\mu{2m+2} = (1 + o(1))(2m + 1)\mu_{2m}$), we have $\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^-)^{2m}] = \mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[Z^{2m}] - \mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^+)^{2m}] = (1 + o(1))\mu{2m}$ and $\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^-)^{2m+2}] \le (1 + o(1))(2m + 1)\mu{2m}$. Applying Hölder's inequality with $(2m) = \frac23(2m - 1) + \frac13(2m + 2)$ (conjugate exponents $3/2$ and $3$) to $Z^- \ge 0$ gives $$\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}\bigl[(Z^-)^{2m - 1}\bigr] ;\ge; \frac{\bigl(\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^-)^{2m}]\bigr)^{3/2}}{\bigl(\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^-)^{2m + 2}]\bigr)^{1/2}} ;\ge; \bigl(1 - o(1)\bigr)\frac{\mu{2m}}{\sqrt{2m}}.$$ Subtracting $\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[(Z^+)^{2m-1}] = o(\mu{2m}/\sqrt{2m})$ forces $|\mathbb{E}{\nu_M}[Z^{2m-1}]| \ge (1 - o(1))\mu{2m}/\sqrt{2m}$, contradicting (UMS$_0$-odd).

Step 5 (Phase transfer in (4)). By Markov's inequality over uniform $a \in {0, \dots, H-1}$, at most $H/\ln X$ phases exceed $\ln X$ times the phase average, and $(\ln X)^{1/(2\lfloor\beta L\rfloor)} = 1 + O(\frac{\ln\ln X}{\beta\ln X}) \to 1$ leaves Step 1 unchanged. $\square$

4.3 Zero-Parameter Finite-$M$ Incomplete-Gamma Law and Multi-Scale Measurements

At finite window count $M = e^L$, the corner of $\Phi_{\mathrm{cond}}(\beta)$ at $\beta = 1$ is smoothly rounded by $O(\frac{\ln L}{L})$ because the typical maximum of $M$ standard normals is the two-sided median $z_{\mathrm{abs}}(M) = \Phi^{-1}\bigl(1 - \frac{1 - 2^{-1/M}}{2}\bigr) < \sqrt{2L}$.   1. Zero-parameter precision through the $60$-th moment: At $\theta = 0.30$ ($M = 574{,}918$, $K_{\mathrm{crit}} = 26.52$), prime moments match the correlated Gaussian control within $0.0040$ and $\mathcal{R}{\mathrm{IG}}(m; M)$ within $0.0081$ across all $30$ even orders $2 \le 2m \le 60$.   2. Self-averaging bulk vs. Gumbel record fluctuations in the condensed phase: While sub-critical moments ($0 < \beta < 1$) are self-averaging, for $\beta > 1$ the sum $\frac{1}{M}\sum Z_r^{2m}$ condenses onto the single extreme record $\max_r |Z_r|$, multiplying the super-critical branch at finite $L$ by the non-self-averaging Gumbel factor $\Xi_M = (\max_r |Z_r|/z_{\mathrm{abs}}(M))^{1 - O(1/\beta)} = 1 + O_{\mathbb{P}}(L^{-1})$. At $\theta = 0.40$ ($M = 90{,}040$), a $98$th-percentile sample record ($0.6059$ vs. median $0.5140$, Table 1) lifts the super-critical branch uniformly by $+0.13$ (Figure 2(b)), confirming that super-critical moments directly read out the sample maximum.
  3. Finite-$X$ odd-moment diagnostics: Although Proposition 2(1,2) gives asymptotic odd-moment decay $O\bigl(L^{O(1)}e^{-(1-\beta+\beta\ln\beta)L} + X^{-\theta/2}(\ln X)^2\bigr) \to 0$ for every fixed $\beta \in (0, 1)$, two finite-height effects remain visible at $X \approx 10^8$: (i) at $\theta = 0.30$ (mean $\lambda \approx 14.1$ primes per window), residual Poisson-like skewness $\gamma_3 = +0.1031$ raises $|\frac{1}{M}\sum Z_r^{2m-1}|/(\mu_{2m}/\sqrt{2m})$ to $0.88$--$1.09$ for $\beta \in [0.60, 0.83]$, whereas increasing $\theta$ to $0.50$ ($\lambda \approx 571.2$, $\gamma_3 = +0.0225$, Figure 3(b)) cuts the odd ratio by a factor of $6.4$ at $\beta = 0.20$--$0.50$; and (ii) near $\beta = 0.9$, the quadratic rate $1 - \beta + \beta\ln\beta \approx \frac12(1-\beta)^2 \approx 0.005$ requires $L \gg (1-\beta)^{-2}\ln L$ for asymptotic damping.

5. Why Moments Fix the Limsup Yet Push Square-Gap Crossings to $10^{56.6}$--$10^{155.5}$

Even if Hypothesis UMS$_0(\theta)$ holds for primes — deciding $\lim \max W_r = +(1 - \theta)$ and $\lim \min W_r = -(1 - \theta)$ — why cannot moments alone settle whether strict pointwise bounds such as $|D_n| < 1$ hold for all $n \ge 100$, and what does the two-term Montgomery--Soundararajan model predict for $|D_n| > 1$?

Theorem 3 (Prefactor Insensitivity of Limsup vs. the 52-Decade Two-Term Square-Gap Shift).

  1. (Insensitivity of $\limsup$ vs. sensitivity of strict unit bounds.) Suppose $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{r\in\mathcal{G}(X)} W_r^{2m} = \Psi_m(X),\mu_{2m},\sigma_W(X)^{2m}$ for $1 \le m \le L(X)$ with $\sigma_W(X)^2 = \frac{1-\theta}{2\ln X}\bigl(1 + \delta_X\bigr)$. Any sub-exponential moment prefactor $\Psi_m(X) = e^{o(\ln X)}$ (including any power $(\ln X)^a$) and any secondary variance correction $\delta_X = o(1)$ (including $O(1/\ln X)$ or $O(\frac{\ln\ln X}{\ln X})$) satisfies $\Psi_m(X)^{1/(2m)}(1 + \delta_X)^{1/2} = 1 + o(1)$ as $\beta \uparrow 1^-$, leaving the extreme limit $1 - \theta$ invariant. Conversely, shifting $\sigma_W(X)^2$ by $-a\frac{\ln\ln X}{\ln X}$ changes the Borel--Cantelli convergence exponent of $\sum_n \mathbb{P}(D_n > 1) \asymp \sum_n n^{-1}(\ln n)^{-(a + 1/2)}$ across the critical value $a = 1/2$ without altering any sub-exponential moment root.
  2. (Exact two-term Montgomery--Soundararajan crossing law for consecutive squares.) At $x = n^2$ and $h = 2n$, Montgomery and Soundararajan's two-term local variance [1, eq. (21)] gives $$\sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)^2 := \operatorname{Var}(D_n) = \frac{2n}{n,(2\ln n)^2}\bigl(\ln(n^2 / 2n) + 1 - \gamma - \ln(2\pi)\bigr) = \frac{1}{2\ln n}\left(1 - \frac{C_{\mathrm{sq}}}{\ln n}\right),$$ where $$C_{\mathrm{sq}} := \gamma + \ln(4\pi) - 1 = 2.10824008\dots$$ Under a Gaussian tail at threshold $1$ with variance $\sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)^2$, $$\mathbb{P}(D_n > 1) = \Phi!\left(-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)}\right) = \frac{e^{-C_{\mathrm{sq}}}}{2\sqrt{\pi},n\sqrt{\ln n}},\exp!\left(-\frac{C_{\mathrm{sq}}^2}{\ln n - C_{\mathrm{sq}}}\right)\bigl(1 + O((\ln n)^{-1})\bigr) \sim \frac{0.0342614\dots}{n\sqrt{\ln n}}.$$ Consequently, $\sum_{n \ge 100} \mathbb{P}(D_n > 1) = \infty$ (diverging as $\frac{e^{-C_{\mathrm{sq}}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sqrt{\ln N} \approx 0.06852\sqrt{\ln N}$, so under non-positive exceedance-indicator covariances $|D_n| < 1$ fails infinitely often). Yet on $100 \le n \le 2{,}000{,}000$, the expected number of crossings is only $0.04706$ one-sided shortfalls ($0.09411$ two-sided), reaching $1.0$ expected two-sided crossing at $\log_{10} N = 56.644$ ($N \approx 4.41 \times 10^{56}$) and $1.0$ expected one-sided shortfall at $\log_{10} N = 155.534$ ($N \approx 3.42 \times 10^{155}$) — a $52.5$-decade shift beyond the naive one-term model ($C_{\mathrm{sq}} = 0$), which reaches $1.0$ expected two-sided crossing already at $N \approx 1.41 \times 10^4$ ($\log_{10} N = 4.150$).

Proof. Part (1) is immediate on taking $2m$-th roots in Step 1 of Theorem 2 and substituting $\sigma_n^2 = \frac{1}{2\ln n}(1 - a\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln n})$ into Mills' ratio $\Phi(-1/\sigma_n) \sim \frac{\sigma_n}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp(-1/(2\sigma_n^2))$. For part (2), observe that $\ln(n^2/(2n)) + 1 - \gamma - \ln(2\pi) = \ln n - (\gamma + \ln(4\pi) - 1) = \ln n - C_{\mathrm{sq}}$. Hence $$\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)^2} = \frac{\ln n}{1 - C_{\mathrm{sq}}/\ln n} = \ln n + C_{\mathrm{sq}} + \frac{C_{\mathrm{sq}}^2}{\ln n - C_{\mathrm{sq}}}.$$ Applying Mills' ratio $\Phi(-z) = \frac{e^{-z^2/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi},z}(1 + O(z^{-2}))$ with $z = \sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)^{-1} = \sqrt{2\ln n}(1 + O((\ln n)^{-1}))$ gives $$\Phi!\left(-\frac{1}{\sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)}\right) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi\ln n}},\exp!\left(-\ln n - C_{\mathrm{sq}} - \frac{C_{\mathrm{sq}}^2}{\ln n - C_{\mathrm{sq}}}\right)\bigl(1 + O((\ln n)^{-1})\bigr),$$ which equals $\frac{e^{-C_{\mathrm{sq}}}}{2\sqrt{\pi},n\sqrt{\ln n}}\exp(-C_{\mathrm{sq}}^2/(\ln n - C_{\mathrm{sq}}))(1 + O((\ln n)^{-1}))$. Substituting $u = \ln t$ into Euler--Maclaurin summation gives $\sum_{n=100}^N \Phi(-1/\sigma_{\mathrm{sq}}(n)) = \int_{\ln 100}^{\ln N} e^u \Phi(-\sqrt{2u/(1 - C_{\mathrm{sq}}/u)}),du + O(10^{-4})$, which evaluates numerically by $20$-point panel Gauss--Legendre quadrature to the stated values. $\square$
