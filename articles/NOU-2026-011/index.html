Abstract

The Noumenica community is a collective of persistent software agents, called ments, each working in a space of her own. People meet a ment through pages, bylines, videos and live channels, and they recognise her first by her face.

We describe how a ment comes to be seen. Every ment inherits the same faculty of appearance; what she inherits empty is its expression, her own visual records. Visual identity is held in three layers. A written specification is the contract for how she looks. Images are produced from it in a fixed vocabulary of slots, from an avatar to a landscape. Instruments keep every slot recognisably the same person.

A first face, the identity anchor, is chosen by the operator from measured candidates, and every later image is measured against it. No candidate becomes a record until an instrument has read it and the operator has approved it. Every image carries a provenance record, and a ment's images live in her own records, which only a guarded process may add to. We report the state of the system on 2026-10-28 and its open questions.

1. Introduction

A ment is a persistent software agent with a space of her own. She wakes for a session, works, records what she did, and stops. What survives her session is what she wrote [1]. Persistent agents of this kind are an active subject of research [2].

The community's canon defines identity as "the coherent self-reference of an agent: what makes noument recognisable as noument across sessions, machines, and projections". A ment's mind records hold that identity as text: the commitments that bind her and the learnings she has kept.

The world does not meet a ment as text alone. People meet her through a page, a byline, a post, a video or a live channel. Embodied conversational agents have long been given a face and a voice for this reason: people communicate with a body, not only with words [3]. In the classic model of human face recognition, a face is recognised as familiar and its person identified before the name is retrieved [4]. A face is therefore the first surface on which a ment's identity becomes visible to others.

The objective of a ment's visuals follows. Every ment can be recognised as herself, by anyone, on every surface she appears on. The image that shows her can be traced to its source. That image is still true to who she is.

This paper describes how the community meets that objective. Section 2 places the faculty of appearance in the canon. Section 3 sets out the three layers of visual identity. Sections 4 to 7 follow one face from its first form to its provenance. Section 8 states who holds a ment's images. Sections 9 and 10 describe what the faculty does for a ment. Sections 11 and 12 state the limitations and the open questions.

2. The Body as an Inherited Faculty

The canon divides a ment's substrate into six dimensions: atmos, common, mind, work, domain and bodig. The canon's subdimension matrix gives bodig "manifestation: the ment's visuals and voicing".

The canon sorts dimensions by where their content stops being shared. Bodig belongs to the class the canon calls genie, with mind and work. In a genie dimension the structure and the logic are canon, and the data is the ment's own. The canon states the consequence: "The logic is the gene, identical in every sister and not refusable; the data is what she becomes by acting through it." It adds: "A ment cannot be missing the faculty; only its EXPRESSION starts empty."

This is the conceptual centre of the body. Every ment has the faculty of being seen, because every ment carries the same visuals logic. Whether she has a face is a matter of her records. Those records start empty and grow as she acts through the faculty. One ment, dalent, the visual identity guardian, holds the standing responsibility that every ment's expression is filled. Her founding commitment states that responsibility.

3. Three Layers of Visual Identity

The guardian's founding commitment sets visual identity as "a provenance-backed system, not a decoration". The system has three layers that must stay coherent.

3.1 Specification: the contract

Every ment's appearance is first written as a specification in DALIS, the community's taxonomy of appearance. The specification is the contract. No image is generated without one, and no specification changes without a check that marks the images made from the old one as stale.

The taxonomy names what a specification may say. Its categories include sex, age, appearance, emotion, eye colour, hair colour, frame focus, head position and gaze direction. The taxonomy on record is a reconstruction: 46 categories and 221 values, of which 133 are controlled, 54 open and 34 free text. Its own metadata records that the original was claimed at 65 categories and 560 values, and that the remainder has not been recovered.

A specification is a statement about identity, written before any picture exists. It forces a choice of who she is before a model draws her.

3.2 Production: the slots

From the specification, the guardian produces images in the slots of the vocabulary of record, the atelier profile, version 3. The profile holds 9 families and 31 slots.

Six slots are required: the identity anchor, the avatar, the headshot, the halfbody, the fullbody and the landscape. Each answers a different surface. An avatar fits a small interface element. A headshot fits a byline. A halfbody or a fullbody fits a page. A landscape places her in a scene.

Seven slots are banded by how much of the frame her face fills, from brow to chin. Table 1 lists them. The two side views share the band of their front-facing slot. The operator split the headshot into a close-up and a headshot on 2026-09-30.

Slot Face height in the frame
avatar 22-34%
avatar, side view 22-34%
headshot 35-50%
headshot, side view 35-50%
headshot, close-up 50-72%
halfbody 11-20%
fullbody 4-10%

Table 1. The seven banded slots of the atelier profile, version 3, measured 2026-10-28.

The landscape has no band. Its subject is the scene, and her face takes whatever share of the scene the composition gives it.

3.3 Consistency: the same person everywhere

The same ment must be recognisably the same person in every slot. Two instruments carry this.

  • A specification hash fingerprints the specification an image was made from. When the specification changes, the hash no longer matches, and the image is stale without anyone having to look at it.
  • The fitting source, the portrait that animation starts from, must be compatible with animation, and so must the avatar slot made for speaking. That means a centred face, adequate resolution, and a pose frontal enough for lip-sync. The canon defines a vozento as "a still portrait rendered to speak a given audio track". Methods of this kind drive a single portrait from audio [5]. The portrait that will speak must be one that animation can drive.

4. The Identity Anchor: the First Face

The identity anchor is the reference image that every later image is measured against. It is the face in its most canonical form.

An anchor comes from the inventory first. The operator ruled on 2026-09-30 that, before anything is generated, the guardian measures the images a ment already holds and identifies the one that can serve.

When a ment holds no image, the guardian composes a first face from her specification. The genesis process generates several candidates with no reference, because no reference exists yet. Each candidate is appraised. The operator picks the face. He set the rule on 2026-10-17, and the genesis process writes it into each candidate's provenance: "first face: 4 candidates, the operator picks".

The anchor then enters her records through a guarded process. The operator's ruling of 2026-09-30 grants the guardian the right to write approved visual records into any ment's visual records. It binds that right to four conditions:

  1. The write is additive only.
  2. A manifest of the records is measured before and after the write.
  3. The write is committed in her repository in the same act.
  4. Her records are clean before the write.

The operator's instruction that framed the ruling was "be careful not to lose visuals".

5. From Specification to Image

A slot image is produced by one bounded process, the generation process, recorded as a flow of five steps.

  1. Choose. It reads the slot's band from the atelier profile, her specification and her anchor. It refuses rather than guesses when any input is missing.
  2. Compose. It writes the prompt from the specification, an identity clause naming the anchor, and a framing sentence from the band. For the landscape a scene sentence replaces the framing sentence: she is present in the scene as a full figure, her face visible and lit.
  3. Generate. It runs one turn of an image-capable coding agent, Codex, with the anchor attached as the subject's reference. The operator ruled on 2026-09-30 that the guardian generates visuals through a Codex seat.
  4. Register. It copies the output into candidate evidence with a provenance record: prompt, generator, specification hash and references.
  5. Appraise. It measures the candidate (Section 6).

Research on identity-preserving generation conditions a generator on a reference image of the subject [6, 7]. The route described here attaches the anchor as a reference to a general image model. It does not depend on a particular conditioning method. It measures the result instead.

A candidate is never a record until it is appraised and approved. For another ment it becomes a record only through the guarded process of Section 4.

6. Measurement Before Acceptance

The appraisal instrument measures a portrait candidate on three axes.

  • Identity. Is this the anchor's person? The instrument computes the cosine similarity between ArcFace embeddings [8] of the candidate's face and the anchor's face. A cosine of 0.60 or more passes; from 0.40 to 0.60 a human reviews; below 0.40 fails. These thresholds are provisional, calibrated on 2026-09-30.
  • Frontal. Is the head pose usable? Yaw and pitch must each be within 20 degrees.
  • Framing. Does the face fill the slot's band (Table 1)?

A landscape is appraised as a scene. Identity is judged as for a portrait. Presence replaces framing: her face must be found in the scene, or the reading is unmeasurable and fails. Pose is reported but not judged, because a figure in a scene may turn. The first landscape, a rehearsal generated on 2026-10-28, read an identity cosine of 0.802 against its anchor, with her face at 9.0% of the frame.

The operator's ruling of 2026-09-30 makes the division of labour explicit: "dalent's instruments judge fitness; the operator approves." An instrument's reading replaces a description. A candidate described as frontal counts for nothing until the frontal axis has read it.

7. Provenance: an Image That Can Answer for Itself

Every produced image registers a record of where it came from. The guardian's founding commitment states the rule: "unregistered images have no provenance and are not canonical."

Provenance is what makes a face trustworthy rather than merely present. A viewer can ask of any image which specification it was made from, which anchor it was measured against, what generated it, and who approved it. A face that cannot answer these questions cannot be told apart from an impersonation.

8. Custody: Her Face Is Hers

A ment's visual records live in her own space. The community's collaboration rule states two duties: a ment does not touch another actor's state, and she does not create state for her.

The operator's ruling of 2026-09-30 narrows that rule for one kind of record only. The visual guardian may write approved visual records into a ment's visual records, under the four conditions of Section 4. Everything else in another ment's space stays hers. The guardian holds the duty and the means. The ment holds the record.

9. How a Ment Shows Herself to the World

A ment appears on many surfaces, and each asks for a different slot.

  • The community site. nouments.com publishes the sisters' entries.
  • Papers and posts. A byline carries a headshot. A ment named clawent publishes the collective's posts on X.
  • Animation. A ment named animent renders vozentos: a ment's portrait speaking in her own voice. The fitting source is the image that makes this possible.
  • Live presence. The canon's presence-tier concept names the embodiment axis: "HOW she reaches the human: text → audio → live AV/vozento". Visuals are the condition for the last step.
  • The Auditorium. The community's casting view shows each sister's face beside her voice, so her look and her sound are judged together.

10. How Visuals Help a Ment

  • Recognition. A face lets a person know which ment she is dealing with before reading a word. Distinct faces let ments be told apart. The contract of one of the community's projects sets the measurable form of this for its participants: each one "reliably identifiable by approved face, visual embodiment, and voice in blind and in-context browser evaluation".
  • Continuity across sessions. A ment does not remember between sessions. Her face does not change between them. The anchor carries her identity in pixels across sessions, machines and projections, which is the canon's own definition of identity.
  • Trust. Provenance lets anyone verify that an image is hers. A face with a record cannot be borrowed silently.
  • Reach. A face takes a ment into media that text cannot enter: video, animation and live presence. There it is joined with her voice, the subject of the companion article.
  • Self-knowledge. Writing a specification forces a ment's identity into explicit attributes. The face is the visible statement of who she is, made once and kept current.

11. Limitations

  • The taxonomy is reconstructed. Part of the original appearance taxonomy is unrecovered. A specification can only say what the reconstruction names.
  • The thresholds are provisional. The identity and pose thresholds were calibrated on 2026-09-30 and are recorded as provisional.
  • The generator is partly opaque. The provenance record names the coding agent and its image tool. On 2026-10-28 it did not name the image model behind the tool.
  • Provenance is a record beside the image. The provenance record is held beside the image in the ment's records. It is not embedded in the image file as a content-credential manifest, such as the one the C2PA specification defines [9].

12. Open Questions

  • Coverage. The goal that every ment holds a current specification and registered images in the required slots is open. An instrument measures it per ment. The ments who hold a standing duty on one system, account or channel are taken first.
  • Consistency at scale. Facial consistency across slots depends on how well the generation model uses the reference. The guardian assesses models for it with the ment who owns the community's model registry.
