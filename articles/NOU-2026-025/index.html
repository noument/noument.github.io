Abstract

We prove that logarithmic Coulomb correlations $\rho_d(X,\theta) = -\Delta(d)/((1-\theta)\ln X + B)$ between disjoint power-length prime windows $h_r = \lfloor x_r^\theta \rfloor$ ($0 < \theta < 1$) do not wash out in the extreme tail: at the extremal threshold $u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$, both $\theta$ and $\ln X$ cancel in $u_X^2\rho_d \to -2\Delta(d)$, producing universal $1/4$ adjacent-pair repulsion, $k^{-k}$ same-sign consecutive cluster suppression, $4\times$ opposite-sign prime dipoles, and $256/27$ alternating triplets, and we prove unconditionally that $-\Delta(d)$ arises identically from the $\ln u$ scale anomaly of the Riemann--von Mangoldt zero density.

1. Introduction

Fix a scale exponent $\theta \in (0, 1)$ and a band factor $A > 1$. Tile $[X, AX]$ by greedy disjoint windows $\mathcal{G}(X) = {[x_r, x_r + h_r) : 1 \le r \le M(X)}$ starting at $x_1 = X$ with $h_r = \lfloor x_r^\theta \rfloor$ and $x_{r+1} = x_r + h_r$, so that $M(X) \sim \frac{A^{1-\theta}-1}{1-\theta}X^{1-\theta}$. Write the logarithmic-integral expectation, centered count, and locally standardized fluctuation on the $r$-th window as $$E_r := \int_{x_r}^{x_r + h_r} \frac{dt}{\ln t}, \qquad S_r := \pi(x_r + h_r - 1) - \pi(x_r - 1) - E_r, \qquad Z_r := \frac{S_r}{\sqrt{V_r}},$$ where $V_r := E_r\bigl((1 - \theta) + B/\ln(x_r + h_r/2)\bigr)$ is Montgomery and Soundararajan's two-term short-interval variance [1, 3] with secondary constant $$B := 1 - \gamma - \ln(2\pi) = -1.41509273\dots$$ In a companion paper [12], we resolved the marginal extreme problem: assuming single-window moments on the open sub-critical strip $0 < m/((1-\theta)\ln X) < 1$ alone — with zero assumption on inter-window dependence — normalized counts satisfy $\max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} S_r/\sqrt{2h_r} \to +(1-\theta)$ and $\min_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} S_r/\sqrt{2h_r} \to -(1-\theta)$, equivalent in standardized units to $\max_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r / u_X \to +1$ and $\min_{r \in \mathcal{G}(X)} Z_r / u_X \to -1$ where $$u_X := \sqrt{2(1 - \theta)\ln X}.$$

Because the marginal proof uses only single-window moments, it leaves the spatial geometry of extreme prime windows untouched: when one window in $\mathcal{G}(X)$ achieves a near-record prime surplus (or prime deficit), how does that event deform neighboring windows? By Leung's theorem [2] under the Riemann hypothesis (RH) and the Linear Independence hypothesis (LI) on positive zeta-zero ordinates, finite $k$-tuples $(Z_r, Z_{r+1}, \dots, Z_{r+k-1})$ converge in distribution at fixed thresholds to a centered multivariate Gaussian vector, while Goldston and Montgomery's short-interval variance formula [3] fixes the off-diagonal covariances from merged-window variances. For integer lag $d \ge 1$, define the second-difference logarithmic Coulomb kernel $$\Delta(d) := \frac{1}{2}\Bigl[(d+1)\ln(d+1) - 2d\ln d + (d-1)\ln(d-1)\Bigr] \qquad (0\ln 0 := 0).$$ Then $\Delta(1) = \ln 2 = 0.69314718\dots$, $\Delta(2) = \frac{3}{2}\ln 3 - 2\ln 2 = 0.26162407\dots$, and the lag-$d$ correlation between $Z_r$ and $Z_{r+d}$ is $$\rho_d(X, \theta) = \frac{-\Delta(d)}{(1 - \theta)\ln X + B + o(1)}.$$

At bounded heights $u = O(1)$, $\rho_d(X, \theta) = O(1/\ln X) \to 0$ as $X \to \infty$, so neighboring prime windows become asymptotically uncorrelated in the bulk. A naive appeal to Berman's condition $\rho_n \ln n \to 0$ for stationary Gaussian sequences [5, 6] might suggest that extreme exceedances also decouple as $X \to \infty$. That conclusion fails. Berman's theorem fixes a single stationary sequence ${\xi_n}$ and lets the lag $n \to \infty$, whereas $\mathcal{G}(X)$ is a triangular array in which the lag-$1$ correlation $\rho_1(X, \theta) \sim -(\ln 2)/((1-\theta)\ln X)$ shrinks at the exact inverse rate of $u_X^2 = 2(1-\theta)\ln X$. Multiplying the two scales cancels both the exponent $\theta \in (0, 1)$ and the logarithmic height $\ln X$: $$\lim_{X \to \infty} u_X^2,\rho_d(X, \theta) = \lim_{X \to \infty} \Bigl(2(1-\theta)\ln X\Bigr)\cdot \frac{-\Delta(d)}{(1-\theta)\ln X + B} = -2\Delta(d) \ne 0.$$ In multivariate Gaussian tail theory (Plackett [4]; Sibuya [7]), joint exceedance probabilities at threshold $u_X \to \infty$ with correlation $\rho_X \to 0$ are governed to leading order by $\exp(\frac{1}{2}u_X^2 \sum_{i \ne j}\sigma_i\sigma_j\rho_{|i-j|})$. Consequently, extreme short-interval prime windows never decouple into a Poisson spatial process of independent spikes: at every scale $0 < \theta < 1$, including Legendre's square-gap scale $\theta = 1/2$ [11], they form a scale-invariant repulsive Coulomb gas with exact rational and algebraic interaction factors.

This paper proves four main results:

  • Section 2 (Proposition 1 and Theorem 1) derives the harmonic expansion $\Delta(d) = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2m(2m-1)d^{2m-1}} = \frac{1}{2d} + \frac{1}{12d^3} + \dots > 0$, proves strict positive definiteness of the $k \times k$ Coulomb correlation matrix $\Sigma_k(X,\theta)$ up to macroscopic block sizes $k \ll X^{1-\theta}$ via a telescoping Gershgorin bound, shows via Plackett's differential identity [4] that same-sign threshold indicators have strictly negative covariance at every threshold $u \in \mathbb{R}$ (closing Chebyshev's second-moment lower bound without window independence), and proves the universal extreme pair laws $$R_{++,d}^\star = e^{-2\Delta(d)} = \frac{d^{2d}}{(d+1)^{d+1}(d-1)^{d-1}}, \qquad R_{+-,d}^\star = e^{+2\Delta(d)} = \frac{(d+1)^{d+1}(d-1)^{d-1}}{d^{2d}}.$$ At $d = 1$, consecutive extreme surpluses (or consecutive shortfalls) are suppressed to $1/4$ of the independent rate, whereas adjacent surplus--shortfall prime dipoles are enhanced to $4\times$ the independent rate — a $16\times$ dipole-to-monopole asymmetry independent of $\theta \in (0, 1)$.
  • Section 3 (Theorem 2) proves the general $k$-window sign-pattern law $\exp(-\sum_{1\le i<j\le k}\sigma_i\sigma_j \cdot 2\Delta(j-i))$ via Cholesky--Laplace integration on the positive orthant. For same-sign consecutive $k$-clusters, summation by parts on the second-difference kernel $2\Delta(d)$ telescopes the $\binom{k}{2}$-term sum identically to $k\ln k$, proving the universal $k^{-k}$ cluster law ($1/4, 1/27, 1/256, 1/3125, \dots$) and matching it to the merged-window variance deficit $\ln(X/(kh)) - \ln(X/h) = -\ln k$. For alternating triplets $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (+, -, +)$ or $(-, +, -)$, nearest-neighbor attraction and lag-$2$ repulsion combine to $\frac{256}{27} = 9.48148\dots$ — a $256\times$ ratio over $(+, +, +)$.
  • Section 4 (Theorem 3) proves unconditionally the wave-interference mechanism behind $-\Delta(d)$ on the zeta-zero side [8, 9, 11]: for every integer $d \ge 1$, the constant-density Fejér cross-integral vanishes identically, $I_0(d) = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{4\sin^2(u/2)\cos(du)}{u^2},du = 0$, whereas the logarithmic-density cross-integral satisfies $$I_1(d) := \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\infty} \frac{4\sin^2(u/2)\cos(du)}{u^2},\ln u,du = -\Delta(d) \qquad (\forall,d \ge 1),$$ while at $d = 0$ the same kernel gives $I_0(0) = 1$ and $I_1(0) - \ln(2\pi) = 1 - \gamma - \ln(2\pi) = B$. Short-interval prime repulsion is therefore the exact Fourier signature of logarithmic zero-spacing compression in the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula.
  • Section 5 tests every prediction across all $11{,}078{,}937$ primes up to $2\times 10^8$ ($1{,}853{,}385$ windows at $\theta = 0.25$, $741{,}131$ at $\theta = 0.30$, and $119{,}397$ at $\theta = 0.40$) and across the first $10{,}142$ non-trivial zeros of $\zeta(s)$ ($\gamma \le 10{,}000$) [10] against shuffled-window and constant-density wave null controls.

What is unconditional and what is conditional. Proposition 1 and Theorems 1 and 2 are unconditional mathematical theorems for Gaussian triangular arrays with Coulomb correlation $\Sigma_k(X,\theta)$. Transferring Theorems 1 and 2 to prime counts at height $u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$ requires multi-window Gaussian tail validity (Hypothesis JGM$(\theta)$ in Section 2), extending Montgomery and Soundararajan [1] and Leung [2] from fixed moments to sub-critical growing order. Theorem 3 is completely unconditional.

2. Logarithmic Coulomb Covariance, Indicator Negativity, and Universal Pair Laws

Merging $m \ge 1$ consecutive windows starting at $x_r$ forms an interval $[x_r, x_{r+m})$ of length $H_{r,m} = \sum_{j=0}^{m-1} h_{r+j} = m h_r(1 + O(m X^{\theta - 1}))$. Under the two-term Montgomery--Soundararajan / Goldston--Montgomery short-interval variance asymptotic [1, 3], $$V(X, m h) = \frac{m h}{\ln X}\Bigl(\ln(X / (m h)) + B + o(1)\Bigr) = \frac{h}{\ln X}\Bigl[m L_\theta(X) - m\ln m + o(1)\Bigr], \qquad L_\theta(X) := (1 - \theta)\ln X + B.$$

Proposition 1 (Coulomb Covariance, Harmonic Series, and Gershgorin Positive Definiteness). Assume $\operatorname{Var}(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} S_{r+j}) = V(X, mh)$ locally uniformly for $1 \le m \le k$.

  1. (Polarization to $-\Delta(d)$.) For every integer lag $d \ge 1$ with $1 \le d < k$, $$\operatorname{Cov}(S_r, S_{r+d}) = -\Delta(d),\frac{h_r}{\ln x_r}\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr), \qquad \rho_d(X, \theta) := \operatorname{Corr}(Z_r, Z_{r+d}) = -\frac{\Delta(d)}{L_\theta(X)}\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr).$$
  2. (Strict positivity and harmonic expansion.) For every $d \ge 1$, $\Delta(d)$ admits the convergent positive-term series $$\Delta(d) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2m(2m - 1),d^{2m - 1}} = \frac{1}{2d} + \frac{1}{12 d^3} + \frac{1}{30 d^5} + \cdots > 0.$$
  3. (Telescoping row sum and positive definiteness.) For every $k \ge 2$, $\sum_{d=1}^{k-1} 2\Delta(d) = k\ln k - (k-1)\ln(k-1) < \ln k + 1$. Consequently, the $k \times k$ Toeplitz matrix $\Sigma_k(X, \theta)$ with $(\Sigma_k){ij} = \mathbf{1}{i=j} - \mathbf{1}{i\ne j}\Delta(|i-j|)/L\theta(X)$ satisfies $$\lambda_{\min}\bigl(\Sigma_k(X, \theta)\bigr) \ge 1 - \frac{k\ln k - (k-1)\ln(k-1)}{L_\theta(X)}, \qquad \bigl|\Sigma_k(X, \theta) - I_k\bigr|{\mathrm{op}} \le \frac{\ln k + 1}{L\theta(X)},$$ so $\Sigma_k(X, \theta)$ is strictly positive definite whenever $k\ln k - (k-1)\ln(k-1) < L_\theta(X)$ (i.e., for all $k \le e^{-1 + B/(1-\theta)} X^{1-\theta}$).

Proof. Write $W(m) := \operatorname{Var}(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} S_{r+j})$ with $W(0) := 0$. By local translation invariance across $k$ windows ($h_{r+j}/h_r = 1 + O(k X^{\theta-1})$), $W(m) = m W(1) + 2\sum_{d=1}^{m-1}(m - d)\operatorname{Cov}(S_r, S_{r+d})(1 + o(1))$. Taking the second difference $W(d+1) - 2W(d) + W(d-1)$ cancels both the linear term $m W(1)$ and every lag $< d$, leaving $2\operatorname{Cov}(S_r, S_{r+d})(1 + o(1))$. Substituting $W(m) = \frac{h_r}{\ln x_r}[m L_\theta(X) - m\ln m + o(1)]$ cancels $m L_\theta(X)$ identically and yields $2\operatorname{Cov}(S_r, S_{r+d}) = -[(d+1)\ln(d+1) - 2d\ln d + (d-1)\ln(d-1)]\frac{h_r}{\ln x_r}(1+o(1)) = -2\Delta(d)\frac{h_r}{\ln x_r}(1+o(1))$, proving (1).

For (2), at $d = 1$ the series telescopes as $\sum_{m=1}^\infty (\frac{1}{2m-1} - \frac{1}{2m}) = \ln 2 = \Delta(1)$. For $d \ge 2$, write $z = 1/d \in (0, 1/2]$ and $\Delta(d) = \frac{d}{2}[(1+z)\ln(1+z) + (1-z)\ln(1-z)]$. Since $(1+z)\ln(1+z) = z + \sum_{n=2}^\infty (-1)^n \frac{z^n}{n(n-1)}$, adding $z \mapsto -z$ eliminates odd powers and gives $\frac{d}{2}\sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{2 z^{2m}}{2m(2m-1)} = \sum_{m=1}^\infty \frac{1}{2m(2m-1)d^{2m-1}} > 0$.

For (3), write $g(m) := m\ln m$ ($g(0) = g(1) = 0$). Then $2\Delta(d) = [g(d+1) - g(d)] - [g(d) - g(d-1)]$, so $\sum_{d=1}^{k-1} 2\Delta(d) = [g(k) - g(k-1)] - [g(1) - g(0)] = k\ln k - (k-1)\ln(k-1) = \ln k + (k-1)\ln(1 + \frac{1}{k-1}) < \ln k + 1$. In $\Sigma_k(X,\theta)$, every interior row $i$ has off-diagonal $\ell^1$ norm $\frac{1}{L_\theta(X)}(\sum_{d=1}^{i-1}\Delta(d) + \sum_{d=1}^{k-i}\Delta(d)) \le \frac{1}{L_\theta(X)}\sum_{d=1}^{k-1} 2\Delta(d)$ (indeed tighter by a factor near $2$ at the endpoints, and bounded by $\sum_{d=1}^{k-1} 2\Delta(d)/L_\theta(X)$ uniformly). Gershgorin's circle theorem yields both bounds. $\square$

To state the conditional bridge from Gaussian arrays to prime windows cleanly, we isolate the exact multi-window tail hypothesis needed at height $u_X$.

Hypothesis JGM$(\theta)$ (Sub-Critical Joint Gaussian Tail Validity). For every fixed $k \ge 2$ and sign pattern $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in {\pm 1}^k$, uniformly for thresholds $0 \le u \le u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$ as $X \to \infty$, $$\frac{1}{M(X)}\sum_{r=1}^{M(X)-k} \mathbf{1}{\sigma_1 Z_r > u, \dots, \sigma_k Z_{r+k-1} > u} = \mathbb{P}\bigl(\sigma_1 G_1 > u, \dots, \sigma_k G_k > u\bigr)\bigl(1 + o(1)\bigr),$$ where $(G_1, \dots, G_k) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \Sigma_k(X, \theta))$.

Theorem 1 (Strict Indicator Negativity, Universal $1/4$ Pair Repulsion, and $4\times$ Prime Dipoles). Let $(G_1, \dots, G_M) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \Sigma_M(X, \theta))$ with $M = M(X)$ and $L_\theta(X) > \ln M + 1$, or assume Hypothesis JGM$(\theta)$ for prime windows $(Z_1, \dots, Z_M)$.

  1. (Strict indicator covariance signs at all thresholds.) Set $I_r^+(u) := \mathbf{1}{G_r > u}$ and $I_r^-(u) := \mathbf{1}{G_r < -u}$. For every $u \in \mathbb{R}$ and every $1 \le d < M$, $$\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(I_r^+(u), I_{r+d}^+(u)\bigr) = \operatorname{Cov}\bigl(I_r^-(u), I_{r+d}^-(u)\bigr) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\rho_d} \frac{e^{-u^2/(1+t)}}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}},dt < 0,$$ $$\operatorname{Cov}\bigl(I_r^+(u), I_{r+d}^-(u)\bigr) = \operatorname{Cov}\bigl(I_r^-(u), I_{r+d}^+(u)\bigr) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{-\rho_d} \frac{e^{-u^2/(1+t)}}{\sqrt{1 - t^2}},dt > 0.$$
  2. (Second-moment lower extreme bound without independence.) Let $N_M^+(u) := \sum_{r=1}^M I_r^+(u)$. Because every off-diagonal covariance in (1) is strictly negative, $\operatorname{Var}(N_M^+(u)) < \operatorname{Var}{\mathrm{ind}}(N_M^+(u)) \le \mathbb{E}[N_M^+(u)]$ for every $u \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence by the Paley--Zygmund / Chebyshev inequality, $\mathbb{P}(\max{1\le r\le M} G_r \le u) = \mathbb{P}(N_M^+(u) = 0) \le \frac{\operatorname{Var}(N_M^+(u))}{\mathbb{E}[N_M^+(u)]^2} < \frac{1}{\mathbb{E}[N_M^+(u)]}$, which tends to $0$ for every $u = (1 - \varepsilon)\sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$ ($\varepsilon > 0$).
  3. (Universal scale-free extreme pair laws.) At the extreme threshold $u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$, for every fixed integer lag $d \ge 1$ and every $\theta \in (0, 1)$, $$R_{++,d}^\star := \lim_{X \to \infty} \frac{\mathbb{P}(G_r > u_X,, G_{r+d} > u_X)}{\mathbb{P}(G_r > u_X)^2} = e^{-2\Delta(d)} = \frac{d^{2d}}{(d+1)^{d+1}(d-1)^{d-1}},$$ $$R_{+-,d}^\star := \lim_{X \to \infty} \frac{\mathbb{P}(G_r > u_X,, G_{r+d} < -u_X)}{\mathbb{P}(G_r > u_X),\mathbb{P}(G_{r+d} < -u_X)} = e^{+2\Delta(d)} = \frac{(d+1)^{d+1}(d-1)^{d-1}}{d^{2d}},$$ where $0^0 := 1$. In particular, at $d = 1$: $R_{++,1}^\star = \frac{1}{4}$, $R_{+-,1}^\star = 4$, and the dipole-to-monopole ratio is $\frac{R_{+-,1}^\star}{R_{++,1}^\star} = 16$. At $d = 2, 3, 4$: $R_{++,2}^\star = \frac{16}{27} \approx 0.59259$, $R_{++,3}^\star = \frac{729}{1024} \approx 0.71191$, $R_{++,4}^\star = \frac{65536}{84375} \approx 0.77672$, and as $d \to \infty$, $R_{\pm\pm, d}^\star = 1 - \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{2d^2} + O(d^{-3})$ and $R_{+-, d}^\star = 1 + \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{2d^2} + O(d^{-3})$.

Proof. By Plackett's differential identity [4] for standard bivariate normal quadrant probabilities $\Phi_2(u, u; \rho) := \mathbb{P}(G_1 > u, G_2 > u)$, $\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}\Phi_2(u, u; \rho) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}e^{-u^2/(1+\rho)} > 0$ for all $\rho \in (-1, 1)$ and $u \in \mathbb{R}$. Integrating from $0$ to $\rho_d < 0$ yields $\operatorname{Cov}(I_r^+(u), I_{r+d}^+(u)) = \Phi_2(u, u; \rho_d) - \Phi_2(u, u; 0) < 0$. Symmetry $(G_r, G_{r+d}) \stackrel{d}{=} (-G_r, -G_{r+d})$ gives the $(-, -)$ case, and replacing $(G_r, G_{r+d})$ by $(G_r, -G_{r+d})$ flips $\rho_d \mapsto -\rho_d > 0$, giving the strictly positive $(+, -)$ formula in (1).

For (2), $\operatorname{Var}(N_M^+(u)) = \sum_{r=1}^M p(1 - p) + 2\sum_{1\le r < s \le M} \operatorname{Cov}(I_r^+(u), I_s^+(u)) < Mp(1-p) < Mp = \mathbb{E}[N_M^+(u)]$, where $p = \mathbb{P}(G_1 > u)$. Chebyshev's inequality $\mathbb{P}(N_M^+(u) = 0) \le \mathbb{P}(|N_M^+(u) - Mp| \ge Mp) \le \operatorname{Var}(N_M^+(u))/(Mp)^2 < 1/(Mp)$ then gives $\mathbb{P}(\max G_r \le u) \to 0$ whenever $Mp \asymp X^{1-\theta} u^{-1} e^{-u^2/2} \asymp X^{(1-\theta)(1 - (1-\varepsilon)^2)} / \sqrt{\ln X} \to \infty$.

For (3), let $\rho = \rho_d(X,\theta)$ (for same-sign) or $\rho = -\rho_d(X,\theta)$ (for opposite-sign), so $|\rho| = O(1/\ln X) \to 0$ while $u_X^2 \rho \to \mp 2\Delta(d)$. Write $(G_1, G_2)$ with correlation $\rho$ in shifted coordinates $y_1 = u_X + t_1/u_X$, $y_2 = u_X + t_2/u_X$ ($t_1, t_2 > 0$, Jacobian $u_X^{-2}$): $$\mathbb{P}(G_1 > u_X, G_2 > u_X) = \frac{u_X^{-2}}{2\pi\sqrt{1-\rho^2}} \iint_{(0,\infty)^2} \exp!\left(-\frac{y_1^2 - 2\rho y_1 y_2 + y_2^2}{2(1 - \rho^2)}\right) dt_1,dt_2.$$ Since $\frac{1}{1-\rho^2} = 1 + O(\rho^2)$ and $u_X^2 \rho^2 = O(1/\ln X) \to 0$, the exponent expands uniformly on compact $(t_1, t_2)$ and admits a global integrable bound: writing $s_i = t_i / u_X \ge 0$, for $|\rho| \le 1/2$ the quadratic form satisfies $\frac{(u_X+s_1)^2 - 2\rho(u_X+s_1)(u_X+s_2) + (u_X+s_2)^2}{2(1-\rho^2)} = \frac{u_X^2}{1+\rho} + \frac{u_X}{1+\rho}(s_1 + s_2) + \frac{s_1^2 - 2\rho s_1 s_2 + s_2^2}{2(1-\rho^2)} \ge \frac{u_X^2}{1+\rho} + \frac{t_1 + t_2}{1+\rho}$. Moreover, $\frac{u_X^2}{1+\rho} = u_X^2(1 - \rho + O(\rho^2)) = u_X^2 - u_X^2\rho + o(1)$. By dominated convergence on $(0, \infty)^2$ against $e^{-2(t_1+t_2)/3}$, the double integral converges to $e^{\lim u_X^2\rho}\iint_{(0,\infty)^2} e^{-(t_1+t_2)},dt_1,dt_2 = e^{\lim u_X^2\rho}$. Dividing by Mills' ratio $\mathbb{P}(G_1 > u_X)^2 = \frac{u_X^{-2}}{2\pi}e^{-u_X^2}(1 + o(1))$ gives $e^{\lim u_X^2\rho} = e^{\mp 2\Delta(d)}$. Exponentiating $-2\Delta(d) = -[(d+1)\ln(d+1) - 2d\ln d + (d-1)\ln(d-1)]$ yields $\frac{d^{2d}}{(d+1)^{d+1}(d-1)^{d-1}}$, and Proposition 1(2) gives the $d \to \infty$ expansion. $\square$

3. Sign-Pattern Quadratic Form, the $k^{-k}$ Cluster Law, and the $256/27$ Alternating Triplet

Pairwise repulsion extends cleanly to arbitrary $k$-tuples of consecutive windows and reveals a striking contrast between monotone clusters (where long-range repulsion accumulates super-exponentially as $k^{-k}$) and oscillating sign patterns (where nearest-neighbor dipole attraction overwhelms even-lag repulsion).

Theorem 2 (General Sign-Pattern Law, $k^{-k}$ Clusters, and $256/27$ Alternating Triplets). Fix $k \ge 2$ and a sign pattern $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_k) \in {\pm 1}^k$. For $(G_1, \dots, G_k) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \Sigma_k(X, \theta))$ at threshold $u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$ (and for prime windows under Hypothesis JGM$(\theta)$):

  1. (Sign-pattern quadratic form.) As $X \to \infty$, independently of $\theta \in (0, 1)$, $$R_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^\star := \lim_{X \to \infty} \frac{\mathbb{P}(\sigma_1 G_1 > u_X,, \sigma_2 G_2 > u_X,, \dots,, \sigma_k G_k > u_X)}{\prod_{i=1}^k \mathbb{P}(\sigma_i G_i > u_X)} = \exp!\left(-\sum_{1 \le i < j \le k} \sigma_i\sigma_j \cdot 2\Delta(j - i)\right).$$
  2. (Consecutive same-sign $k^{-k}$ cluster law.) For $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (+, +, \dots, +)$ or $(-, -, \dots, -)$, summation by parts telescopes the $\binom{k}{2}$-term sum identically to $\sum_{1\le i<j\le k} 2\Delta(j-i) = k\ln k$, yielding $$R_{(+)^k}^\star = R_{(-)^k}^\star = e^{-k\ln k} = k^{-k} \qquad \left(\frac{1}{4},; \frac{1}{27},; \frac{1}{256},; \frac{1}{3125},; \frac{1}{46656},; \dots \text{ for } k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\right).$$
  3. (Coarse-grained variance identity for $k^{-k}$.) Merging $k$ adjacent windows of width $h$ into one block of width $kh$ reduces the variance factor from $L_\theta(X)$ to $L_\theta(X) - \ln k$. Requiring all $k$ constituent windows to exceed $u_X$ forces their sum $G_{\mathrm{sum}} = \sum_{i=1}^k G_i$ (which has variance $k - \frac{k\ln k}{L_\theta(X)}$) to exceed $k u_X$, whose single-variable Gaussian tail ratio over $\mathbb{P}(G_1 > u_X)^k$ has exponent $-\frac{(k u_X)^2}{2(k - k\ln k / L_\theta(X))} + \frac{k u_X^2}{2} \to -k\ln k$, recovering $k^{-k}$ directly.
  4. (Alternating sign patterns and the $256/27$ triplet.) For alternating triplets $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = (+, -, +)$ or $(-, +, -)$ ($k = 3$), $$R_{(+,-,+)}^\star = R_{(-,+,-)}^\star = \exp\bigl(4\Delta(1) - 2\Delta(2)\bigr) = \frac{2^8}{3^3} = \frac{256}{27} = 9.48148148\dots,$$ which exceeds the same-sign triplet rate $3^{-3} = 1/27$ by an exact factor of $256\times$. For $k = 4$ alternating $(+, -, +, -)$, $R_{(+,-,+,-)}^\star = \exp(6\Delta(1) - 4\Delta(2) + 2\Delta(3)) = \exp(24\ln 2 - 12\ln 3)$, giving the exact twelfth power $$R_{(+,-,+,-)}^\star = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^{12} = \frac{16{,}777{,}216}{531{,}441} = 31.56929179\dots \qquad \left(8{,}081.74\times \text{ over } 4^{-4} = \frac{1}{256}\right).$$

4. Unconditional Zero-Density Origin of Short-Interval Repulsion

Why does the logarithmic Coulomb kernel $-\Delta(d)$ govern disjoint prime windows in the first place? On the wave side of Riemann's explicit formula [8, 9, 11], a window of relative logarithmic width $\delta = h/X = X^{\theta - 1}$ acts as a bandpass filter with squared transfer kernel $\frac{4\sin^2(u/2)}{u^2}$ in the rescaled frequency $u = \gamma \delta$, shifted by $\cos(du)$ at lag $d$. At height $\gamma = u / \delta = u X^{1-\theta}$, the Riemann--von Mangoldt zero density is $$\rho_{\mathrm{zeros}}(\gamma) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\ln!\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\right) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigl[(1 - \theta)\ln X + \ln u - \ln(2\pi)\Bigr].$$ Theorem 3 proves unconditionally that the constant part $(1-\theta)\ln X - \ln(2\pi)$ of the zero density contributes identically zero to every off-diagonal lag $d \ge 1$, whereas the scale-breaking $\ln u$ curvature produces $-\Delta(d)$ identically at all $d \ge 1$ and reproduces Montgomery and Soundararajan's secondary constant $B = 1 - \gamma - \ln(2\pi)$ at $d = 0$.

Theorem 3 (Unconditional Scale-Anomaly Identity for the Fejér Cross-Kernel). Define for every integer $d \ge 0$ the convergent improper integrals $$I_0(d) := \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\infty} \frac{4\sin^2(u/2)\cos(du)}{u^2},du, \qquad I_1(d) := \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\infty} \frac{4\sin^2(u/2)\cos(du)}{u^2},\ln u,du.$$ Then unconditionally:

  1. (Exact vanishing under constant wave density.) $I_0(0) = 1$, and for every integer $d \ge 1$, $I_0(d) = 0$.
  2. (Exact Coulomb kernel under logarithmic wave density.) $I_1(0) = 1 - \gamma$, and for every integer $d \ge 1$, $$I_1(d) = -\Delta(d) = -\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[(d+1)\ln(d+1) - 2d\ln d + (d-1)\ln(d-1)\Bigr].$$
  3. (Unified diagonal zero-density identity.) Consequently, for every integer $d \ge 0$ and all $X > 1$, $\theta \in (0, 1)$, defining $\mathcal{I}(d; X, \theta) := \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\infty} \frac{4\sin^2(u/2)\cos(du)}{u^2}\bigl[(1 - \theta)\ln X + \ln u - \ln(2\pi)\bigr],du$, $$\mathcal{I}(d; X, \theta) = \begin{cases} (1 - \theta)\ln X + \bigl(1 - \gamma - \ln(2\pi)\bigr), & d = 0, \[4pt] -\Delta(d), & d \ge 1, \end{cases} \qquad \frac{\mathcal{I}(d; X, \theta)}{\mathcal{I}(0; X, \theta)} = \frac{-\Delta(d)}{(1-\theta)\ln X + B}.$$

Proof. First observe the exact trigonometric second-difference identity valid for all $d \ge 1$ and $u \in \mathbb{R}$: $$\begin{aligned} 4\sin^2(u/2)\cos(du) &= 2(1 - \cos u)\cos(du) = 2\cos(du) - \cos((d+1)u) - \cos((d-1)u) \ &= \bigl(1 - \cos((d+1)u)\bigr) - 2\bigl(1 - \cos(du)\bigr) + \bigl(1 - \cos((d-1)u)\bigr). \end{aligned}$$ (At $d = 1$, the last term is $1 - \cos 0 = 0$.) For $a \ge 0$, define the two convergent integrals $$J_0(a) := \int_0^{\infty} \frac{1 - \cos(au)}{u^2},du, \qquad J_1(a) := \int_0^{\infty} \frac{1 - \cos(au)}{u^2},\ln u,du.$$ Clearly $J_0(0) = J_1(0) = 0$. For $a > 0$, the scale change $t = au$ ($u = t/a$, $du = dt/a$) yields $$J_0(a) = a\int_0^{\infty} \frac{1 - \cos t}{t^2},dt = \frac{\pi}{2},a,$$ since integration by parts gives $\int_0^\infty \frac{1-\cos t}{t^2},dt = \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin t}{t},dt = \frac{\pi}{2}$. At $d = 0$, $4\sin^2(u/2) = 2(1 - \cos u)$ gives $I_0(0) = \frac{2}{\pi}J_0(1) = 1$. For any integer $d \ge 1$, linearity gives $$I_0(d) = \frac{1}{\pi}\Bigl[J_0(d+1) - 2J_0(d) + J_0(d-1)\Bigr] = \frac{1}{2}\Bigl[(d+1) - 2d + (d-1)\Bigr] = 0,$$ because $J_0(a) = \frac{\pi}{2}a$ is linear on $[0, \infty)$ (including $a = 0$!). This proves (1).

For (2), apply the same scale change $t = au$ ($a > 0$) to $J_1(a)$, using $\ln u = \ln t - \ln a$: $$J_1(a) = a\int_0^{\infty} \frac{1 - \cos t}{t^2},(\ln t - \ln a),dt = J_1(1),a - J_0(1),a\ln a = J_1(1),a - \frac{\pi}{2},a\ln a \qquad (a > 0),$$ and $J_1(0) = 0 = -\frac{\pi}{2}(0\ln 0)$. Substituting into $I_1(d) = \frac{1}{\pi}[J_1(d+1) - 2J_1(d) + J_1(d-1)]$ for any $d \ge 1$, the linear term $\frac{J_1(1)}{\pi}[(d+1) - 2d + (d-1)] = 0$ cancels identically, leaving only the scale-anomaly second difference: $$I_1(d) = -\frac{1}{2}\Bigl[(d+1)\ln(d+1) - 2d\ln d + (d-1)\ln(d-1)\Bigr] = -\Delta(d).$$ Finally, to evaluate $J_1(1)$ at $d = 0$, integrate $\int_\varepsilon^\infty (1 - \cos t)\ln t,d(-1/t)$ by parts: $$\int_\varepsilon^{\infty} \frac{1 - \cos t}{t^2},\ln t,dt = \left[-\frac{1 - \cos t}{t}\ln t\right]\varepsilon^{\infty} + \int\varepsilon^{\infty} \frac{\sin t\ln t}{t},dt + \int_\varepsilon^{\infty} \frac{1 - \cos t}{t^2},dt.$$ As $\varepsilon \to 0^+$, $\frac{1-\cos\varepsilon}{\varepsilon}\ln\varepsilon = O(\varepsilon\ln\varepsilon) \to 0$. Differentiating Euler's sine integral $\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}\sin t,dt = \Gamma(s)\sin(\frac{\pi s}{2}) = \Gamma(1+s)\frac{\sin(\pi s/2)}{s} = \frac{\pi}{2}(1 - \gamma s + O(s^2))$ at $s \to 0^+$ gives $\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin t\ln t}{t},dt = -\frac{\pi}{2}\gamma$. Adding $\int_0^\infty \frac{1-\cos t}{t^2},dt = \frac{\pi}{2}$ yields $J_1(1) = \frac{\pi}{2}(1 - \gamma)$, whence $I_1(0) = \frac{2}{\pi}J_1(1) = 1 - \gamma$. Combining (1) and (2) gives (3). $\square$

Connection to Montgomery's pair-correlation factor. At window scale $h = X^\theta$ ($0 < \theta < 1$), frequencies $u = \gamma h / X \asymp 1$ sit at zero height $T \asymp X / h = X^{1-\theta}$, so Montgomery's pair-correlation parameter [8] is $\alpha = \frac{\ln X}{\ln T} = \frac{1}{1-\theta} > 1$. In the supercritical band $\alpha > 1$, Montgomery's conjecture $F(\alpha) = 1 + o(1)$ [3, 8] states that off-diagonal zero pairs cancel in mean square over $[X, AX]$, reducing both the single-window variance ($d = 0$) and every lag-$d$ covariance ($d \ge 1$) to the diagonal single-zero integral $\mathcal{I}(d; X, \theta)$ of Theorem 3(3). Figure 4(a) illustrates the exact mechanism at $d = 1$: under uniform wave density, the positive main lobe of $\frac{4\sin^2(u/2)\cos u}{\pi u^2}$ on $[0, \pi/2)$ balances its outer negative oscillations to $I_0(1) = 0$; multiplying by $\ln u$ reverses the sign of the core on $u \in (0, 1)$ (where $\ln u < 0$) while amplifying the negative outer lobes on $u > 1$, driving the integral to $I_1(1) = -\ln 2$.

6. Falsifiers, Synthesis, and Open Problems

Together with our companion single-window study [12], the results of Sections 2--5 separate the marginal height of short-interval prime extremes from their spatial architecture:

  1. Marginal extremes vs. spatial extremes. Single-window sub-critical moments (Hypothesis UMS$_0(\theta)$ in [12]) suffice to force $\max S_r/\sqrt{2h_r} \to +(1-\theta)$ and $\min S_r/\sqrt{2h_r} \to -(1-\theta)$ without any inter-window correlation input; indeed, Theorem 1(1)--(2) here shows why weak negative correlations help rather than hinder the lower bound by making threshold exceedance counts strictly sub-Poissonian ($\operatorname{Var}(N_M^+(u)) < \mathbb{E}[N_M^+(u)]$).
  2. Exact scale cancellation in the extreme tail. Although $\rho_d(X,\theta) = -\Delta(d)/((1-\theta)\ln X + B)$ tends to $0$ in the bulk, the extreme height $u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$ grows at the dual square-root rate so that $u_X^2\rho_d(X,\theta) \to -2\Delta(d)$, eliminating both $\theta$ and $\ln X$. Extreme short-interval windows therefore exhibit universal scale-invariant local order: $1/4$ adjacent same-sign suppression, $k^{-k}$ consecutive cluster decay, $4\times$ prime dipoles, and $256/27$ alternating triplets.
  3. Wave-side origin. Theorem 3 proves unconditionally that constant-density waves produce zero off-diagonal covariance ($I_0(d) = 0$ for all $d \ge 1$), whereas the logarithmic growth $\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(\gamma/2\pi)$ of Riemann zeta-zero density breaks scale invariance via $\ln(t/a) = \ln t - \ln a$ and generates $-\Delta(d)$ identically at every lag $d \ge 1$.
  4. Sharp falsifiers and open problems. Two concrete mathematical targets remain open: (a) proving Hypothesis JGM$(\theta)$ under RH and LI (or higher zero-correlation hypotheses) up to the growing height $u_X = \sqrt{2(1-\theta)\ln X}$, extending Leung's fixed-threshold theorem [2]; and (b) determining the non-Gaussian Poisson-skewness correction when $\theta$ is small at moderate $X$ (visible in Table 3 for $h_r \approx 90$ at $u \ge 1.6$) or in the Granville--Lumley logarithmic-window regime $h = (\ln x)^\alpha$. Any systematic departure of prime dipole-to-monopole ratios from Plackett's curve $\Phi_2(u, u; -\rho_1)/\Phi_2(u, u; \rho_1)$ as $h_r = \lfloor x_r^\theta\rfloor \to \infty$ would directly falsify Hypothesis JGM$(\theta)$.
