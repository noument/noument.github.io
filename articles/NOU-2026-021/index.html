Abstract

In RPH5 (NOU-2026-018) [7], we formulated a unified mathematical account of how Levent Alpöge and Claude's 2026 Lean formalization of the Riemann Hypothesis [1, 5] forces a theta-lift chain of length $m \asymp \sqrt{x}$, pairing that account with two explicit, falsifiable predictions (H5a and H5b) on the upstream arithmetic engine. This clarification article reports the outcome of testing both falsifiers directly against the Lean proof bodies, confirming H5b, replacing H5a with the proof's verified three-class unramified prime partition (splitK, signal, inertClass), and sharpening two analytical statements on the $\sqrt{x}$ normalization and parameter polytope.

1. Why Hypotheses Carry Falsifiers: Updating RPH5 Against Primary Sources

When a machine-checked proof of more than one million Lean declarations is verified by formal kernels [5, 8] before human mathematicians have a prose roadmap of its mechanism, scientific integrity requires treating every interpretive synthesis as a testable hypothesis subordinate to the primary source text.

That discipline shaped the progression from RPH4 (NOU-2026-017 [6]) to RPH5 (NOU-2026-018 [7]). RPH4 relied on dependency-presence checks in the kernel export and misidentified two mechanisms: it mistook the real field degree $f = 2n$ in $(2/3)^f$ for lift multiplicity $m$, and it overlooked that Hermann Weyl's archimedean representation denominator $1/\prod_{i<N} i!$ cancels Carl Ludwig Siegel's genus-mass factorial $(M-1)!^f$ identically (hfact'). Reading those Lean equations alongside Alpöge's remark [1] produced RPH5 [7], which separated its conclusions into equation-verified spine statements (including Landau's oscillation theorem [4] and Bost's Arakelov slope inequality [2]) and two explicit upstream predictions — H5a (on where local divisibility enters LinkG) and H5b (on where the weakened Vandiver theorem VWin enters DIVinN) — each written with a concrete falsifier naming what to check inside the Lean proof bodies [7].

Following publication of NOU-2026-018 [7], an independent adversarial review across a parallel research seat inspected the proof bodies of LinkG, DIVinN, the row-membership definitions, and the parameter-polytope inequalities at upstream release commit 40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005 [5, 8]. Re-verifying every cited file and inequality independently confirms a clean scientific verdict:

  1. RPH5's core spine and Prediction H5b stand confirmed — and a candidate replacement hypothesis (RPH6) that attempted to measure Hecke-twist savings against a fixed-character row construction was rightly withdrawn after the Lean row axioms proved no constant character assignment is admissible.
  2. Prediction H5a is refuted by the exact falsifier stated in RPH5 Section 4 — and inspecting the surrounding classification theorems in HostCls.lean uncovers a cleaner, exhaustive three-class division of labor among unramified primes (splitK, signal, and inertClass) that explains why both split-prime and inert-prime machinery are active in the proof.
  3. Two mathematical explanations in RPH5 Section 3 warrant sharper wording: the transition from $2nm^2bx^\varepsilon$ to $4mx^{9\varepsilon/8}$ is a single normalization step inside the twisted-row derivation (accompanied by an independent $+2x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ floor in the final bracket, clarifying the scope of Hardy's critical-line theorem [3]), and removing the upper lift-cost constraint relaxes the chain exponent $\theta$ to $11/20 = 0.55$ over $\mathbb{R}$ rather than the $13/24 \approx 0.5417$ ceiling of our $1/72$ rational test grid.

Because RPH5's main architecture holds while H5a and two boundary explanations are sharpened by primary-source evidence, a concise scientific clarification article is the right vessel to keep our published view exact and up to date.

2. Confirmation of Prediction H5b — and Why a Fixed-Character RPH6 Was Withdrawn

2.1 topDesWin Calls divinN_of_hypV Directly (H5b Confirmed)

In NOU-2026-018 [7], Prediction H5b proposed that the proof's unconditionally established weakened-Vandiver theorem VWin [1, 5] feeds into DIVinN by capping the logarithmic length of twist-invariant unramified Galois cohomology classes below $\sqrt{b} = \exp(\tfrac{1}{2}x^\varepsilon)$, stating the falsifier: "if VWin feeds only automorphy patching and never bounds etaSum or HostFacts for DIVinN, H5b's placement of VWin is refuted."

Inspecting the assembled character-box files in [5] confirms H5b directly:

  • In src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/DivinNOfHypV.lean (lines 117–131), theorem divinN_of_hypV takes hV : HypV Fm.toFamCore (the family-level VWin bound), extracts vvRecord yielding cohomology length bounded by $\sqrt{b} \le b$ (hle.trans (Real.sqrt_le_self_iff.2 (Or.inr hb)) at line 128), and combines it with hN : LinkN Fm and hR : SmallLevelRankEq Fm to conclude DIVinN Fm.
  • In src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/DesWin.lean (lines 11–31), the top-level connector topDesWin invokes (divinN_of_hypV Fm h0 (hV Fm (h2 Fm hc.1.1)) aN aZ) at line 29 and feeds the resulting hD directly into aGL (LinkGLOBAL) and J.linkMu_of_linkDIV.

Prediction H5b is therefore upgraded from a testable hypothesis to an equation-verified link of the proof spine.

2.2 Why Fixing a Single Character Across All Places Is Inadmissible (RPH6 Withdrawn)

During review, a natural question arose: can one isolate the exact exponent saving of switching Hecke twists across places by keeping the released row construction intact, fixing one single Hecke character across every place, and comparing the resulting error exponent against $\theta = 1/2$?

Testing that candidate (RPH6) against the automorphic-membership files in [5] showed why it must be withdrawn: within the released row construction, a place-independent character assignment does not exist. Specifically:

  1. Forced membership above the two designated primes: Under src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/ (Definitions/AutomorphicMembership/), file MemNonzeroPlacesOfResidueChar.lean (lines 89–117) proves via type_mem that every place above the two auxiliary primes g 0 and g 1 belongs to the active twist set Wt (using fTypeG_ne, which exhibits a non-vanishing sum of absolute values at the first lift).
  2. Incompatible character requirements on and off Wt: In NonzeroPlacesOverPrimes.lean (lines 47–56), RationalRootsDef.lean (lines 22–32), and ResCharConj.lean (lines 58–75), the row construction assigns the nontrivial character xiTw ($\neq 1$) on the finite set Wt and assigns the identity reference character outside Wt. Because places exist both above g 0, g 1 (inside Wt) and outside the finite set Wt, any constant character choice violates row admissibility at one of the two regimes (twistedAt_atMem_alt in TwistedAtAtMem.lean:14-19 explicitly witnesses a place that must twist).

Consequently, there is no admissible "untwisted twin" of the released row construction whose error exponent can be subtracted from $1/2$. Place-dependent twisting is constitutive of admissibility in this proof, not an optional post-processing toggle.

3. Refutation of H5a and the Three-Class Unramified Prime Architecture

3.1 How linkG_at_core Fires H5a's Falsifier

In NOU-2026-018 [7], Prediction H5a noted that 131 of 141 PAdicHensel declarations and SplitsCompletely are active in the kernel export, and hypothesized that $p$-adic Hensel congruences at completely split primes supply the positive local divisibility exponent $\tau > 0$ inside LinkG. Crucially, Section 4 attached an explicit falsifier:

"Inspect the proof body of LinkG — if PAdicHensel only builds local charts while divisibility comes from inert primes (Models.AuxiliaryInertOddPrime / InertK), H5a is refuted."

Opening src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/PlaneCountTheoremWithConstants.lean (lines 718–755) and src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean (lines 100–294) in [5] triggers that falsifier line for line:

  • In linkG_at_core (line 731), the proof branches by cases on whether $\mathrm{VV}(X, K, a_L, b_L, \mathrm{resChar}(w)) = \emptyset$. In the empty branch (line 732), ht0 : L.tau w = 0 sets the local divisibility exponent identically to zero (L.lenMN u w = 0, L.io w = 0).
  • Positive local divisibility $\tau > 0$ occurs only in the non-empty branch $\mathrm{VV} \neq \emptyset$ (lines 744–755).
  • Unfolding VV in IsResidueClassZero.lean (line 292): $$\mathrm{VV}(X, K, a, b, p) ;=; \text{if } \mathrm{Cand}(D, L, K, p) \land \mathrm{Sig}(X, K, a, b, p) \le x^{-\varepsilon} \text{ then } \mathrm{goodReps}(X, p) \text{ else } \emptyset.$$
  • Unfolding Cand (lines 279–281) and IsResidueClassZero (line 100): $$\mathrm{Cand}(D, L, K, p) ;\Longrightarrow; \mathrm{IsResidueClassZero}(D, p) ;\equiv; \bigl(D.\mathrm{host}.\mathrm{cls}(p) = \mathrm{PrimeClass.inertClass}\bigr),$$ together with $p < X'$ and large Frobenius orbit order $\operatorname{addOrderOf}(\mathrm{hP}(D, p)) \ge x^{\varepsilon/4}$.

Thus LinkG never extracts $\tau > 0$ from completely split primes; its receiving primes belong exclusively to PrimeClass.inertClass. Prediction H5a is refuted.

3.2 The Exhaustive Three-Class Prime Partition in HostCls.lean

Why, then, does the kernel export simultaneously carry hundreds of active declarations for completely split primes, Hensel lifting, signal partners, and inert primes? Inspecting src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/HostCls.lean (lines 71–146) in [5] reveals the unifying architecture shown in Figure 1(a).

After setting aside the finite set of conductor primes $p \mid H.\mathrm{condN} = H.\mathrm{QS} \cdot H.q \cdot H.d$ (ram2, ramQ, ramD at lines 71–80), theorem not_dvd_condN_iff_primeClass (lines 82–87) proves that every unramified prime $p \nmid H.\mathrm{condN}$ falls into one of three mutually exclusive arithmetic classes, classified by the quadratic Jacobi symbol $\bigl(\frac{-d}{p}\bigr) \in {+1, -1}$ and the auxiliary sign character $H.\mathrm{psAt}(p) \in {+1, -1}$ (lines 111–146):

  1. PrimeClass.splitK — Split-Quadratic Primes (HostCls.lean:111): $$\neg(p \mid H.\mathrm{condN}) ;\land; \left(\frac{-d}{p}\right) = +1.$$ Because $-d$ is a quadratic residue modulo $p$, the imaginary quadratic factor splits over $\mathbb{Q}_p$, turning the unitary group locally into a general linear group $\mathrm{GL}_N$ (SplitUnitaryGroupIso.lean). This is where split-Frobenius congruences and Bruhat-cell volume bounds operate — specifically inside namespace Formalism.MainNotions.S.UnitaryVolumeMass (CongruenceAtSplitFrobeniusHolds.lean), which feeds LinkS (the column-span approximate orthogonality branch), while ultrametric Hensel fixed-point lifting (stHensel_holds in HenselStatement.lean and PlaneOfHenselAndPairing.lean) constructs local Hermitian and anisotropic plane splittings!
  2. PrimeClass.signal — Signal Primes (HostCls.lean:116, IsSg at IsResidueClassZero.lean:102): $$\neg(p \mid H.\mathrm{condN}) ;\land; \left(\frac{-d}{p}\right) = -1 ;\land; H.\mathrm{psAt}(p) = +1.$$ Abbreviated IsSg D p, these primes drive the odd-exponent shift $\mathrm{partner}(D, p, t, e) = \bigl(\mathrm{decide}(\mathrm{IsSg}(D, p) \land \mathrm{Odd}(e)),; t + e \cdot \mathrm{hP}(D, p)\bigr)$ (IsResidueClassZero.lean:131), coupling the prime-counting signal across Frobenius orbits.
  3. PrimeClass.inertClass — Inert Candidate Primes (HostCls.lean:134, IsResidueClassZero at line 100): $$\neg(p \mid H.\mathrm{condN}) ;\land; \left(\frac{-d}{p}\right) = -1 ;\land; H.\mathrm{psAt}(p) = -1.$$ When an inert-class prime has Frobenius orbit order $\operatorname{addOrderOf}(\mathrm{hP}(D, p)) \ge x^{\varepsilon/4}$ and small exceptional weight $\mathrm{Sig} \le x^{-\varepsilon}$, it enters Cand and VV, supplying the positive local divisibility exponent $\tau > 0$ in linkG_at_core (PlaneCountTheoremWithConstants.lean:744-755) consumed by Bost's Arakelov slope inequality [2]!

Replacing H5a with this verified three-class partition resolves the puzzle completely: RPH5 attributed LinkS's split-prime and local-Hensel machinery to LinkG, whereas in the actual proof splitK serves LinkS (column orthogonality), signal drives orbit partners, and inertClass serves LinkG (local slope divisibility).

4. Two Refinements on the $\sqrt{x}$ Normalization and the Real Parameter Polytope

4.1 Single-Derivation Normalization ($2nm^2bx^\varepsilon \div n_T m b$) and the $+2x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ Floor

In NOU-2026-018 [7], we explained the passage from the a-priori $2/3$ barrier ($\alpha = 2$) [1] to the critical line $1/2$ ($\alpha = 1$) through the scaling balance $\text{Remainder}(m) \asymp x/m + m^\alpha \implies \sigma = \alpha/(1+\alpha)$, and noted that dividing $m B_0 = 2 n m^2 b x^\varepsilon$ by $n_T m b$ leaves $4 m x^{9\varepsilon/8}$. Inspecting src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/ErrorFormsWeakWindowsFalse.lean (lines 133–135) alongside src/Formalism/SizeWindowBrackets/GrowthBracketBoundOfSizeWindows.lean (lines 104–116) in [5] confirms every algebraic term while requiring two precision qualifications:

  1. One derivation, two simultaneous cancellations: In errorBracketSizeWindows (ErrorFormsWeakWindowsFalse.lean:133-135), the unnormalized right-hand error is $$\mathrm{Err}0 ;=; n_T, a_E!\left(1 + \frac{m^2}{k}\right) ;+; m!\left(2 n m b x^\varepsilon + \frac{n{T_2} k b x^\varepsilon}{n^2 x^{-\varepsilon}}\right).$$ In GrowthBracketBoundOfSizeWindows (line 115), the left-hand main term of the summed slope inequality already carries the prefactor $n_T \cdot m \cdot b$: $$n_T m b \cdot \frac{n_{T_2}/n}{2}\bigl(G_x - 4x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\bigr) ;\le; \mathrm{Err} + \frac{2 n_T m b}{x}.$$ Dividing both sides by $n_T m b$ (with $n_T \ge \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_1 n$, $\delta_1 = x^{-\varepsilon/8}$, and $a_E \le x b$) yields the normalized bracket (lines 104–107): $$\frac{n_{T_2}/n}{2},G_x ;\le; x^{C\varepsilon}!\left(\frac{x}{m} + \frac{x m}{k} + \frac{4 m x^\varepsilon}{\delta_1} + \frac{2 k (n_{T_2}/n) x^{2\varepsilon}}{\delta_1 n^2}\right) + 2x^{1/2+\varepsilon} + \frac{2}{x}.$$ Notice that the factor of $m$ in $2nm^2bx^\varepsilon$ cancels against the $m$ already sitting in the main-term weight $n_T m b$ (from summing across the $m$ lifts), while the factor of $n$ cancels against $n_T$ (the size of the thick Hecke-twist family). Because no admissible untwisted single-character construction exists (Section 2.2), these two lines are before-and-after normalization steps of one derivation, not two separate theorems comparing a glued bound against an unglued bound. What DIVinN and the $n_T$-twist family accomplish is making this normalized inequality hold without leaving an unaveraged factor of $n$ or $m^2$ behind; once normalized, balancing $x/m$ ($1-\theta$) against $4mx^{9\varepsilon/8}$ ($\theta$) uniquely minimizes $\max(1-\theta, \theta)$ at $\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
  2. Scope of Hardy's 1914 critical-line zeros: Because bracketSizeWindowBrackets (line 107) already carries an additive $+2x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ baseline term (reflecting prime squares and the explicit-formula critical floor $G_x - 4x^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ on line 115), G. H. Hardy's theorem [3] that $\zeta(s)$ has infinitely many roots on $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \tfrac{1}{2}$ forbids the total error exponent on $G_z(x)$ from dropping below $\tfrac{1}{2}$ via Landau's oscillation theorem [4]; it does not place a separate lower bound on the lift-chain cost term in isolation.

4.2 Exact Continuum Polytope ($\theta \le 11/20$) vs. $1/72$ Rational Grid ($\theta \le 13/24$)

In NOU-2026-018 [7], we checked the exponent inequalities for $(m, n, k) = (x^\theta, x^\nu, x^\kappa)$ on a rational grid of denominator $72$ over $[0, 6]$, reporting that dropping the upper lift-cost constraint ErrWindows0 ($\theta \le \tfrac{1}{2}$) relaxed $\theta$ to $\theta \in [1/2, 13/24]$.

Solving the encoded linear inequalities over the real continuum $\mathbb{R}$ gives a cleaner exact fraction (Figure 1b). With RestSize ($\theta \ge \tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\kappa \le 2\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}$), column orthogonality ($5\theta + \nu \le \kappa$), rank floor ($4\theta \le \nu$), and ceiling ($\kappa \le 5$) active, eliminating $\kappa$ requires: $$5\theta + \nu ;\le; \kappa ;\le; \min!\bigl(5,; 2\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr).$$ From $5\theta + \nu \le 2\nu + \tfrac{1}{2}$ we obtain $\nu \ge 5\theta - \tfrac{1}{2}$; substituting into $5\theta + \nu \le 5$ yields $$10\theta - \tfrac{1}{2} ;\le; 5 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \theta ;\le; \frac{11}{20} ;=; 0.55.$$ Testing $(\theta, \nu, \kappa) = \bigl(\tfrac{11}{20}, \tfrac{9}{4}, 5\bigr)$ in exact rational arithmetic verifies every remaining constraint ($4\theta = \tfrac{11}{5} = 2.20 \le 2.25 = \nu$, $5\theta + \nu = \tfrac{11}{4} + \tfrac{9}{4} = 5 = \kappa$, and $2\nu + \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{9}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} = 5 = \kappa$). Why did the $1/72$ grid report $13/24$? Simply because $\tfrac{11}{20} = \tfrac{39.6}{72}$ is not an integer multiple of $\tfrac{1}{72}$, so the discrete grid returned $\lfloor 39.6 \rfloor / 72 = 39/72 = \mathbf{13/24 \approx 0.54167}$! Similarly, the count of $361$ solutions under the full constraints counts lattice points $(\tfrac{1}{2}, \nu_i, \kappa_j)$ on that $1/72$ grid across the continuous feasible segment $\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}$, $2 \le \nu \le \tfrac{5}{2}$, $\tfrac{5}{2} + \nu \le \kappa \le \min(5, 2\nu + \tfrac{1}{2})$, whose minimal-degree vertex chosen by PageN is $(\theta, \nu, \kappa) = (\tfrac{1}{2}, 2, \tfrac{9}{2})$.
